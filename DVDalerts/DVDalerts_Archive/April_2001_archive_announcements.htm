April DVDALERTS

April 26, 2001

Here's an excellent kids' film for you. Indian in the Cupboard (7/3/01) is outstanding fantasy with some fine lessons in morality to be gleaned from the material. The 1995 film is directed by Frank Oz and adapted from the outstanding children's book. Columbia releases in a special edition with audio commentary from Oz.

Slick, quick, sophisticated fare from France, Diva (6/12/01) gets a DVD polish in a new anamorphic release from Anchor Bay. The 1981 film is directed by Jean-Jacques Beineix. Anchor Bay includes an interview with the director as part of the DVD release.

Paramount steers Wayne's World (7/10/01) onto DVD in a special edition. Mike Myers leads the madness and director Penelope Spheeris delivers an audio commentary for the 1992 film. Wayne's World 2 (7/10/01), the 1993 follow-up, can be purchased as part of a box set or individually. Director Spheeris once again provides audio commentary.

April 21, 2001

Joan Crawford was often gloriously over the top in her screen appearances. Mommie Dearest ( 7/17/01), the acrimonious screen biography of the actress stars Faye Dunaway doing her best to one up the acting style of Crawford. Definitely fun to watch. The 1981 flick is coming from Paramount.

Director George Stevens made films with style and elegance. A Place in the Sun (7/17/01), the director's 1951 drama starring Elizabeth Taylor and Montgomery Clift gets a special edition DVD release from Paramount with audio commentary by the director's son, George Stevens, Jr. and a retrospective interviews of Taylor, Shelly Winters, Stevens, Jr. and associate producer Ivan Moffat.

April 20, 2001

Russell Crowe does his thing in the South American jungle to bring home Meg Ryan's husband in Proof of Life (6/19/01). Warner makes it a special edition with a making of documentary and audio commentary from director Taylor Hackford.

Get out your handkerchiefs weeper fans. Two Douglas Sirk directed films starring Rock Hudson are getting the special edition treatment from Criterion. The 1955 All That Heaven Allows (6/5/01) and the 1956 Written on the Wind (6/5/01) get the widescreen anamorphic enhanced for 16 x 9 treatment.

April 19, 2001

The latest eccentric effort by the Coen Brothers O Brother,  Where Art Thou? (6/12/01) is coming to DVD from Buena Vista. George Clooney leads the crazy convicts on a skewed odyssey.

The highly regarded new comedy from writer/director David Mamet, State and Main (6/19/01) takes on the movie-making process with a light deft touch. New Line is delivering the DVD with audio commentary from stars William H. Macy, Sarah Jessica Parker, David Paymer, Patti Lupone and Clark Gregg.

April 17, 2001

It's easy to believe Tom Courtenay was fantasizing about Julie Christie in Billy Liar (6/19/01). The 1963 film,  a breakthrough for Christie, is coming from Criterion  replete with audio commentary by director John Schlesinger, Tom Courtenay and Julie Christie.

June just may be Criterion month. Included in their flood of releases is My Man Godfrey (6/19/01), the classic zany 1936 starring William Powell and Carole Lombard. The DVD release includes audio commentary by film historian Bob Gilpin, rare outtakes, and a Lux Radio Theater adaptation starring Powell and Lombard.

 The 1958 hilarious Italian comedy Big Deal on Madonna Street (6/5/01) takes burglary bumbling to new heights in Criterion's DVD release. Beautiful black and white and optional English subtitles.

Criterion is releasing another classic foreign film in a special edition, Michaelangelo Antonioni's 1960 L'Avventura (6/5/01) gets the special edition treatment including audio commentary  and a 58 minute documentary.

April 7, 2001

The very moving film from director Vittorio De Sica, The Garden of the Finzi-Contini (6/19/01) debuts on DVD from Columbia. Winner of the 1971 Academy Award© for Best Foreign Film.

Getting a second DVD life from MGM, Black Robe (7/10/01), Bruce Beresford's exquisite film set in Colonial Canada is being re-leased anamorphic widescreen. Beautifully photographed and acted, this story of a missionary priest in 17th century Canada is consistently fascinating.

Slipping through the cracks of release Hell and onto DVD is Michael Winterbottom's The Claim (6/26/01), his 2000 reworking of Erich Von Stroheim's Greed. Certainly worth a look. Anamorphic 2.35 widescreen.

Buena Vista is set to release Malena (7/10/01), Giuseppe Tornatore's latest directing effort. The director's whimsical and sentimental style often treats movie goers to a refreshing point of view.

Nick Nolte gives a smoldering performance in Who'll Stop the Rain (7/10/01) as Vietnam veteran Ray Hicks in the Karel Reisz 1978 adaptation of Robert Stone's Dog Soldiers. Take the drugs and run! Widescreen 1.85, and apparently not anamorphic. Too bad. 

Voted Best Foreign Film of 2000 at the Oscars©, Crouching Dragon, Hidden Tiger (6/5/01) bows on DVD in what promises to be a spectacular looking DVD. Elegant production values help this essentially Hong Kong romantic actioner break out into the American mainstream.

Director Robert Zemeckis takes on the chores of audio commentary for Castaway (6/12/01), with Tom Hanks providing an Oscar© nominated central performance.
