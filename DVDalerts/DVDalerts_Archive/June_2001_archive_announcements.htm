June DVDALERTS

June 14, 2001

Kramer vs. Kramer (8/28/01) arrives on DVD from Columbia. The 1979 drama earned Academy Awards© for Dustin Hoffman as Best Actor  and Meryl Streep as Support Actress. Best Picture, Best Director and Best Screenplay completed the Kramer award romp. Included with the anamorphic widescreen release is a making of documentary.

Columbia is repricing a slew of DVDs to $14.95 retail on August 28, 2001.  Watch for titles like The Fisher King, Five Easy Pieces, The China Syndrome and more.

MGM is dipping deep into the catalogue with  the release of the 1937 black and white adventure King Solomon's Mines (9/4/01). Cedric Hardwick stars as the great white hunter. Now, how about the dashing color 1950 remake with Stewart Granger and Deborah Kerr.

June 12, 2001

A terrific tight little Burt Lancaster Western, Valdez is Coming (9/4/01)is headed to DVD from MGM. The 1970  will be presented widescreen 1.66.

The fine 1983 drama about journalists covering the war in Nicaragua, Under Fire (9/4/01) gets an anamorphic widescreen release from MGM at budget retail price of $14.95. Nick Nolte and Gene Hackman star under Roger Spottiswoode's direction.

The Princess Bride (9/4/01), the way it should have been done the first time out on DVD, has been announced as a special edition by MGM. Audio commentaries from Rob Reiner and William Goldman and a documentary, exactly as you wish the 1987 film delivered  anamorphic widescreen.

Gary Cooper plays Melody Jones in the easygoing 1945 western spoof, Along Came Jones (9/4/01). The black and white film is coming from MGM.

Tom Selleck star in the delightful western Quigley Down Under (9/4/01) coming to DVD from MGM. The 1990 oater is being released widescreen 2.35, but not anamorphic unfortunately. Part of the MGM budget $14.95 line. Quigley, get out your Sharps.

June 11, 2001

Finally, the announcement from Paramount that one of the most significant DVDs missing in action, The Godfather (10/9/01) is coming to DVD together with its two sequels in power packed special edition box set. Packaged together with the 1972 Francis Coppola directed film are the two other legs of the Godfather trilogy, the Godfather Part II  (1974) and Part III (1990). Highlights of the special edition set includes  a separate director's commentary by Coppola on The Godfather, The Godfather Part II and The Godfather Part III. The first and third films in the collection will be contained on a single disc; The Godfather Part II will be on two discs. The fifth disc, exclusive to the box set, includes a 73 minute documentary and more.

June 6, 2001

Universal takes the wraps off their archive of Mummy magic and pulls out the Frankenstein stitches with a baker's dozen of horror films for DVD release on 8/28/01. Packaged two titles to a DVD, excellent sequels like Dracula's Daughter (1936) and Son of Frankenstein (1939) are included in the release wave.

A special edition DVD from Universal of the 1962 Cape Fear (9/18/2001) features a making of documentary. For those interested in making comparisons, the 1991 Cape Fear (9/18/01) remake gets a more thorough examination in yet another special editon, including cast interviews, deleted scenes and a documentary.

The 1984 debut feature of the Coen Brothers, Blood Simple (9/18/01) joins the ever growing DVD catalog from Universal.

June 1, 2001

Acclaimed Chinese filmmaker Hou Hsiao-Hsien has two films arriving on DVD from Winstar. The Puppetmaster (8/14/01), Hou's 1993 film, traces the life of famous puppet master Li Tien-Lu. Flowers of Shanghai (8/14/01), made in 1998,  is set in 19th century Shanghai in the smoky world of a brothel. The Puppetmaster is being released full frame and Flowers  is widescreen 1.85.

Joining Hannibal (8/21/01) at the DVD dinner table is a re-release of Silence of the Lambs (8/21/01) in a special edition. 1991's Silence is released in separate Anamorphic widescreen and pan and scan editions. A double dipping of Anthony Hopkins as Hannibal Lector.

Interesting independent feature from 1990, All the Vermeers in New York (7/31/01), looks at New York City with cool passion. Directed by Jon Jost, the film is being released by World Artists in anamorphic widescreen 16 x 9 enhanced.

Harry Palmer returns as the slightly seedy British Intelligence agent in Funeral in Berlin (8/14/01). Paramount delivers the Len Deighton story anamorphic 2.35. Michael Caine breaths life into Palmer.

Columbia is releasing a special edition of Fly Way Home (8/7/01). This is the first time the lovely 1996 Carroll Ballard film will be anamorphic widescreen enhanced for 16 x 9 on DVD. Audio commentary by Ballard and cinematographer Caleb Dechanel plus a separate commentary from composer Mark Isham with isolated score form the core of the extras.
