May DVDALERTS

May 25, 2001

Harry Palmer returns as the slightly seedy British Intelligence agent in Funeral in Berlin (8/14/01). Paramount delivers the Len Deighton story anamorphic 2.35. Michael Caine breaths life into Palmer.

Columbia is releasing a special edition of Fly Way Home (8/7/01). This is the first time the lovely 1996 Carroll Ballard film will be anamorphic widescreen enhanced for 16 x 9 on DVD. Audio commentary by Ballard and cinematographer Caleb Dechanel plus a separate commentary from composer Mark Isham with isolated score form the core of the extras.

May 24, 2001

Everything's coming up Gump. Forrest Gump (8/28/01) ambles onto DVD carrying a box of special edtion chocolates courtesy of Paramount. Audio commentary, a "making of,"  and more. Tom Hanks mines simplicity to perfection in this 1994 winner.

The 1981 Best Foreign Language Film Academy Award© winner, Mephisto (6/26/01) debuts on DVD from Anchor Bay. Klaus Maria Brandauer is the actor devoured by his art. Interviews with Brandauer and director Istvan Szabo.

May 18, 2001

Buena Vista plans to satisfy the DVD sweet tooth with a special edition release of Chocolat (8/7/2001) including audio commentary from director Lasse Hallstrom. 

Finally two Preston Sturges classics are getting their DVD due from Criterion in special editions. Sullivan's Travels (7/10/01), the 1942 comedy odyssey includes audio commentary from Noah Baumbeck, Christopher Guest and Michael McKean and an interview with Sandy Sturges, the director's widow. The Lady Eve (7/10/01) gets audio commentary from film scholar Marian Keane.  The 1942 comic delight stars Henry Fonda and Barbara Stanwyck.

Columbia is releasing Pollack (7/24/01) in a special edition. Ed Harris's 2000 labor of love should play well on the DVD palette.

May 13, 2001

It's really out there,: i am referring to Monkeybone (7/10/01), a wild comedy ride that proved difficult to pigeon-hole. The 2001 flick is being released in a special edition by Fox, including audio commentary by director Henry Selick and 10 deleted scenes. Why not take the trip "downtown" with star Brendan Fraser.

May 10, 2001

Good news for thriller fans. Brian DePalma's outstanding 1981 Blow Out (8/28/01) bows on DVD from MGM in an anamorphic widescreen transfer. Are you listening John Travolta?

Perhaps De Palma's kinkiest exercise in the thriller genre, Dressed to Kill (8/28/01) gets dressed for DVD in anamorphic clothes by MGM. The 1980 is getting the special edition treatment featuring a 45 minute making of documentary.

August may just be declared  De Palma month. MGM is rolling out another special edition from the director's oeuvre, the 1976 horror flick Carrie (8/28/01). A forty-five minute making of and other extras accompany the film.

May 3, 2001

The world of Atom Egoyan is web spun of icy strands. The director's 1989 Speaking Parts (6/26/01)  arrives on DVD from Zeitgeist including audio commentary from Egoyan.

Director Egoyan strays from his typical Canadian setting with Calendar (6/26/01), his 1993 film set in Armenia. The special edition includes director's audio commentary and a behind the scenes "making of."

Rainer Werner Fassbinder's last film, the 1982 Querelle (7/10/01) arrives courtesy of Columbia in anamorphic widescreen.

Don Siegel's intelligent western,  The Shootist (7/24/01) stars John Wayne in a role that parallels his real life health problems. Paramount delivers the goods for the 1©976 film  in anamorphic widescreen, including some cast and crew interviews.

John Wayne plays big game hunter in the 1962  Howard Hawks production of Hatari (7/24/01). The scenery and wildlife photography compete with the characters and drama for prime audience attention. Anamorphic widescreen.

Clint Eastward and Lee Marvin warble their way through the old west in Paint Your Wagon (7/24/01), Paramount's big budget 1969 musical. They compete for the ladies and the gold but it's happily the only musical for either actor. Anamorphic widescreen.

May 3, 2001

Freaks (6/12/01), at long last, Freaks!  One of those classic horror films that plants itself under your skin like a persistent worm is finally making it to DVD. Music Video is delivering the 1932 Todd Browning classic. Cross your fingers that they found good source material for this release.

Look out world. Takeshi Kitano is on the loose. In his 1995 flick Gonin (7/3/01), Takeshi teams up with four other down and outers to pull off a heist. Ronin anyone? Coming from new DVD player Leo.

George Hamilton picks up the sword with a bit of extra flare in Zorro, the Gay Blade (7/10/01), the 1981 comedy riding in from Image. Zorro fights evil dressed in anamorphic enhanced for 16 x 9 images.

Look for an enthusiastic audio commentary from director Richard Donner accompanying the widescreen anamorphic release of Goonies (8/21/01). Warner adds some deleted footage tot he special edition package all bundled anamorphic widescreen.

 

Slick, quick, sophisticated fare from France, Diva (6/12/01) gets a DVD polish in a new anamorphic release from Anchor Bay. The 1981 film is directed by Jean-Jacques Beineix. Anchor Bay includes an interview with the director as part of the DVD release.

Paramount steers Wayne's World (7/10/01) onto DVD in a special edition. Mike Myers leads the madness and director Penelope Spheeris delivers an audio commentary for the 1992 film. Wayne's World 2 (7/10/01), the 1993 follow-up, can be purchased as part of a box set or individually. Director Spheeris once again provides audio commentary.
