3 Days of the Condor/B+,B+

Paramount/1975/117m/ANA 2.35

     Director Sydney Pollack brings his urbane style of filmmaking to an outstanding spy thriller. Crisp and ruthless editing echoes the plot. You can sit on the edge of your favorite home theater seat as CIA bookworm Joseph Turner slips through an Agency massacre.
     Turner’s the last link to the hit on the CIA section. The Agency is trying to bring him in; the guys with the black gloves are one step behind him. Turner can’t trust anyone but he won’t survive on his own.
   

Not just another nice domestic scene ©Paramount

 Robert Redford is innocent and dashing as Turner. It may be a little hard to believe how easily Turner adapts to the spy game, but Redford makes it believable. Faye Dunaway lights up the screen exquisitely as the girl Turner hides out with. There’s great chemistry between these two stars. Max Van Sydow is truly menacing as an open-minded operative with Cliff Robertson as the Agency contact trying to bring Turner in from the cold.
     Paramount is proving itself a top contender for consistent DVD honors. 3 Days of the Condor is delivered in another outstanding anamorphic transfer that belies the 1975 origin of the feature. Images, while not up to the freshness the state of the art The Mummy, are consistently pleasing and sharp. Color is slightly subdued but otherwise accurate. The details of Redford’s complexion are ably displayed on the screen while a subtle softness follows every close-up of gorgeous co-star Dunaway.
