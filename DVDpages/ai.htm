A.I. (SE)/
DVD Review

 

Teddy's got the audience. ©Dreamworks

    Man, Haley Joel Osment has amazing facial control. I don't think I caught him in a blink once. This kid could win a staring contest with anyone. 

    David is a poorly programmed Mecha/robot considering many of his actions. He does a lot of things a robot shouldn't do. Is it all because he's programmed to love or is it the first indication that the robots are evolving to a degree beyond human control? 

The robot seduction scene is really funny. Gigolo Joe, an updating of the scarecrow in The Wizard of Oz, is probably too garish a creation and I am not sure if the humor adds or detracts from the film. Considering all the fairy tale references are we too believe AI is also a fairy tale? I wonder what The Wizard of Oz would be like if Spielberg made it today. Would it still have the wondrous innocence? 

    Cinematography and lighting design are very interesting. Some extraordinary color combinations are used. 

For a story like this to really succeed, you just have to believe it. It never managed to capture me with its wonder. Perhaps it's a bit too artificial. I guess there are Descartian questions to answer. 

The ending is absolutely beautiful. It surprised me. I almost wanted that one last moment to last forever, just as David wants that day to last forever. 

   I think Spielberg's varied homages to his own visual style in other films works very well. Incredible detail in the forest scene. 
     Consistently tight grain patterns. Slightly washed out look that replicates Spielberg's screen vision. Perhaps there's a touch of grain over-activity here and there, but grain is a tough element to control on DVD. There's no edginess whatsoever. It's very filmlike indeed. Good depth of image. Color control is very good. The birthday party scene looks fabulous. Hyper real colors take over from the mostly dream-like look of the interiors. The transfer is very natural looking. Subtlety rather than power is the keyword here. Great look in the dark street with fog and lights retaining their individuality. Wonderful blacks with fine details on the leather outfit of Gigolo Joe. The DTS surround sound is open and enveloping with all the qualities of a first rate transfer.
