American President/ A-,
DVD Review

A-, B+

US/1995/Color/Widescreen 2.35:1/Stereo Surround/114 minutes/Directed by Rob Reiner/Starring Michael Douglas, Annette Bening/Col-Tristar/58 Chaps/CLV/$34.95

 

The American President is an absolutely stunning romance set in The White House starring two genuine movie stars, Michael Douglas and Annette Bening. It’s that mystery of movies that the screen chemistry missing between Warren Beatty and his wife Bening in Love Affair is happily abundant in this film.

Andrew Shepherd, the youthful, popular widowed president, is dominated by the business of running the country. A savvy politician, he understands where and when to compromise. His focus is well aimed at his objectives. It’s running the country as usual until lobbyist Sydney Ellen Wade enters his life. She’s as irresistible to Shepherd as The American President is to audiences. Political repercussions resound as the script takes some shots at American Politics in the best Capraesque spirit. There’s a delicious moment at The White House Gate when Capra gets his official nod from writer and director.

Director Rob Reiner guides his stars with a sure hand. Douglas is comfortably presidential and at his charming best. The lovely Annette Bening sparkles with intelligence and sex appeal playing the lady with Washington ambitions. And Reiner works old style Hollywood magic by assembling a supporting cast that delivers in every scene appropriately reminiscent of those romantic comedies of the forties in which wonderful casts brought a world to screen life. Michael J. Fox has rarely been used to better effect than as presidential advisor/speechwriter Lewis Rothchild and Martin Sheen delivers solidly as chief of staff A.J. MacInerney. Rounding out the superlative support are Richard Dreyfuss as a right wing senator with presidential aspirations, Samantha Mathis as the president’s secretary, David Paymer as  the pollster and stalwart John Mahoney as Wade’s boss.

Written by Aaron Sorkin in close collaboration with director Reiner, the script is witty and clever enough to avoid being obvious or cliched. Reiner’s sense of pacing is terrific. Each aspect of the film is timed to perfection. Even the political aspirations of the film are not heavy-handed. Adding to the luster is production design that features perfect replication of the interiors of The White House and a beautifully even photographic approach by top pro John Seale. The American President is a terrific team effort.

The American President is a very pleasing laser disc. The image, slightly warm, is an appealing romantic palette. Details are very well defined, lightening presented with natural contrasts. The score by Mark Shaiman is presented in a clean recording. The charm and romance of this film, all too rare these days, will make splendid repeat entertainment.
