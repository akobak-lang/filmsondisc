Dave/ B+,
Warner

Dave B+, A-
US/1993/Color/Widescreen 1.85:1/Stereo Surround/110 minutes/Directed by Ivan Reitman/Starring Kevin Kline, Sigourney Weaver/Warner Home Video/27 Chaps/CLV\$34.98
Dave is a highly successful entry in the political satire sweepstakes. Though it takes broad aim at the abuses of our political system, it never loses its amiable spirit and that is the key to its success.
Kevin Kline stars as Dave Kovic, the owner of a " temp" employment agency, who is a dead ringer for President Bill Mitchell, and is recruited by Mitchell staffers to stand in as the President's double. When Mitchell's Presidency is paralyzed by his untimely massive stroke, Dave is asked to do dare-devil double duty, passing himself off as the President under the full scrutiny of everyone, including Mitchell's wife. The opportunities for farce and satire are copious.
Kline's charm, enthusiasm and inherent innocence make him a natural for Dave. Whether calmly ushering accountant friend Murray into the White House for a pastrami dressed hilarious quick fix conference on the national budget, or being tongue-tied in the presence of the First Lady, Kline is a delight.
The supporting cast is likewise appealing and uniformly excellent . Sigourney Weaver's cynically icy First Lady melts under Dave's spell and Frank Langella is wonderful in an apoplectic turn as the President's megalomaniac Chief of Staff. Kevin Dunn and Ving Rhames are effective as press secretary and secret serviceman respectively, and Ben Kingsley is hysterical as the mild mannered Vice President.
Despite its comic clothing, the underlying social messages in Dave are out front. The question of waste in the budget is deliciously addressed in Dave's "impromptu" solving of the a budget problem at a cabinet meeting. The question of ethical economic priorities versus political fiefdoms is thoroughly lambasted. Though far-fetched in its conception, a thread of fear runs through Dave concerning the possibilities for manipulation of power. 
However, any preaching never gets in the way of the fun in this highly pleasing film from director Ivan Reitman. Reitman keeps the laughs coming and the situation evolving at a quick, yet unhurried, clip. Dave earns a well deserved broad based appeal and a hearty endorsement from my point of view.
The disc looks just grand. It is another quality transfer from Warners. The colors are natural and the contrast ratios pleasing to the eye. Detail is sharply defined both aurally and visually.
