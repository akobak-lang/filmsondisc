Devil's Backbone/ A, A-
Columbia/2001/

      What an elegantly written film. It takes place during the Spanish Civil War in the late thirties. At an orphanage for children of the war, a ghost stalks the grounds, a ripe sexual tension pervades the dusty surrounds, and greed explosively changes everything forever. Great characters, magnificent photography and a solid, if slightly enhanced, transfer from Columbia.

     The script by Guillermo del Toro, Antonio Trashorras, and David Muñoz

Powerful perverse sexuality is coupled with political awareness. Character relationships are highly complex. There's fascinating material buried underneath the shadow of the ghost tale.

Ghostly challenge. ©Columbia

     In sonorous tones the narrator incants over powerful images: "What is a ghost? A tragedy condemned to repeat itself time and again? An instant of pain, perhaps? Something dead which still seems to be alive? An emotion suspended in time like a blurred photograph, Like an insect trapped in amber."

     The special edition includes audio commentary by director Guillermo Del Toro and cinematographer Guillermo Navarro. Listening to Del Toro provides an opportunity to watch Devil's Backbone on another level. Del Toro establishes a firm knowledge of movie-making. Both of these men love movies. That comes through clearly in the commentary. This is a generous commentary with technical details and film philosophy taking equal time.
