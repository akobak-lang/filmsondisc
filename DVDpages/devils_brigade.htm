Devil's Brigade/ B, D+
MGM/1968/131/WS 2.35

    William Holden is the mortar around which The Devil's Brigade is built.  Holden, virtually always a strong presence in his films, delivers once again as Lt. Colonel Robert Frederick. He's got the wait of authority and belief in himself as the determined commanding officer of the newly formed hybrid unit of Americans and Canadians.
     Frederick is one tough man. He doesn't give ground to anyone for anything. He's a leader to admire. The colonel not only has to deal with the tension building amongst his men, but the brass throws him a curve or two. Frederick never flinches, though more than once his ability is questioned because he has no combat experience.

Gentlemen, meets the Canadians. ©MGM

   The first section of the film features a standard treatment of putting together and training a new unit. There's competition between the Americans and Canadians. There's bonding between the men. When the brigade is sent to Italy there's one very entertaining attack that plays equally for laughs and tension; and a second attack designed to perfection with a dramatically big scope.   
     Director Andrew V. McLaglen gives the action a rousing treatment. there are some enjoyable fights and a smattering of barracks humor. The varied character each do their part to add color to the production.
     Holden is joined by Cliff Robertson as the Canadian commander, Vince Edwards as the street tough American Major, and host of familiar supporting players like Richard Jaeckel, Claude Akins, Harry Carey, Jr. and Carroll O'Connor.
    Unfortunately, The Devil's Brigade is bedeviled by unsightly twittering edge artifacts in a majority of the scenes. It's either a lack of intelligent filtering or the master is perhaps composite. Mine is not to reason why, mine is but to buy and sigh. The transfer is surely sharp enough and color saturation is decent. The elements are pretty clean with a few errant marks here and there. A few scenes are slightly soft.
    If you can avoid the minefield of edge artifacts, The Devil's Brigade is a solid war action flick.
