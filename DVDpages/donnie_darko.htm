Donnie Darko (SE)/ B,B
Fox

   Very funny dinner table scene, well timed dialogue, but the message that something's amiss is clearly there.

A rather enigmatic film. It's not easy to make a judgment on the direction it takes. Is the film about a schizophrenic young man? Is the film about adjusting to society? The outsiders looking in? Is it a science fiction movie? Only schizophrenia makes sense in the end.

One long Halloween. ©Fox

     Some of the stylized photography seems like it is trying to carry the story while nothing is really happening.   

Gyllenhaal reminds me of a young Pacino, especially in the Fear/Love class room scene. He's outstanding. A fine talent. He's an accomplished young actor. He adds a level of complexity to Donnie. He's not predictable. And Donnie's a kind individual, a great strength of the film.

The decision to make Donnie's outer space pal wear a rabbit costume is rather bizarre. At times, it makes it laughable, and this film does not want to be laughable. This is no Elwood P. Dowd talking to an invisible rabbit named Harvey; this is a young man going through a very traumatic time in his life.

This is not by-the-numbers filmmaking. The script and direction take on very individual taste and tones of the creative forces behind the film. The production elements are first-rate. The photography is excellent, even the effects are blended quite transparently.

The introduction of the old women going to the mail box every and Donnie is just about the only person who stops to talk to her and share that moment of life with her.

 

Image is a bit inconsistent. Some interiors could have been a bit sharper. Contrast range appeared pinched. It washes out the shadow detail slightly. It's not bad, but not as good as could have been.
