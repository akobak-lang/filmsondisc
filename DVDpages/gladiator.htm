Gladiator (SE)/B+,A-
Dreamworks/2000/

    Welcome back the big epic. Hail Scott! Gladiator chaulks up visual splendor than fallen bodies. The production is the star. The screen is filled with magnificent images, varied lighting schemes, with no let up from start to finish. The script, while quite good, is not in the same league. For a third of the movie your propped up by Roman swords under you seat, but the political intrigue doesn't deliver the same thrust. Gladiator is best when the sword aims for revenge.

There are some issues that still confuse me. It's obvious that Maximus had an affair with Lucilla, knew Commodus as a young, andd according to Marcus Aurelius, has never been to Rome.

 

With all due deference to another 2000 Oscar nominated Best Picture, Gladiator's leaping tigers are a lot more formidle than any crouching tigers.

Do digital tigers bite? ©Dreamworks

The fact that Richard Harris was not Oscar© nominated for his mesmerizing perfomance as Marcus Auerilius is mind boggling. Harris has such an astounding presence in this film that his presence is alive and well some two hours after his death. And Joachim Phoenix nominated int he supporting category. Commodus is unequivocably a leading role, expertly played despite my lack of love for Phoenix on screen. I mourn the fact that the excellent Connie Neilsen did not get to put a fire under masses of male libidos in Gladiator.

I am with you about Harris. He is bloody amazing in the small role in Gladiator. More Harris, more Harris. Yeah, I loved Harris in This Sporting Life. Crowe is such a cold fish. It was magnificent for The Insider, but less so for Gladiator. Works in Romper Stomper too. Gladiator was outstanding, but there were too many instances of excessive edge enhancement. Gladiator was a tad less than perfect, but a great watch
