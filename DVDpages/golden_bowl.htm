Golden Bowl/B+, A-
DVD Review

     The Golden Bowl is Merchant/Ivory filmmaking distilled to a new purity. The settings are still spectacular capturing romantic Italy or sophisticated England in the late nineteenth century, but character portrayals have been pared done to an essential few, riveting the cool Henry James dialogue mostly to a quartet of center-piece players.
    I confess: I came to The Golden Bowl with low expectations. The last time Nick Nolte dressed up for Merchant Ivory was as Thomas Jefferson in that lamentable misfire Jefferson in Paris. The filmmaking team seemed to be losing touch with its audience. What a delightful surprise that everything clicked in The Golden Bowl, and Nolte, so out of place in Jefferson's knickers, was an excellent choice as "America's first billionaire," Adam Verver.
    So much is said between and underneath the surface of the dialogue. The repressed straightforwardness and extreme delicacy in which matters of the heart are handled plays perfectly under James Ivory's clear-sighted direction. Ruth Prawer Jhabvala's screenplay is a paradigm of literary adaptation.

The highly literate Merchant/Ivory adaptation of Henry James' Golden Bowl has been brought to sparkling life on DVD from Lion's Gate. The scintillating color and breathtaking detail is on brilliant display in the grand ball room scene. Check out the DVD. It might take you by surprise.
