House of Mirth
DVD Review

    High-toned drama with a capital H, The House of Mirth plays more like a novel than a film. Dialogue is delivered in clipped, cold tones, and each character is given far too much space and time to complete every thought. Set in New York at the end of the 19th century, the adaptation of the Edith Wharton novel is the story of the very modern Lily Bart's fall from grace.
   What is it about Gillian Anderson that makes me so unsympathetic to her Lily. Perhaps it's the foolish pride that drives her character. I think Ms. Anderson does a fine job, but the cold style of director Terrence Davies undermines her chances to appeal to audiences in more ways than one. The rest of the characters are uniformly cardboard.
