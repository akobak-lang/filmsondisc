Jumanji
DVD Review

 

 

Jumanji

B+, B+

US/1995/Color/Widescreen 1.70:1/Stereo Surround/ 104 minutes/Directed by Joe Johnston/Starring Robin Williams, Bonnie Hunt/Col-Tristar/  Chaps/CLV/THX/$39.95

 

Breathlessly directed by Joe Johnston, Jumanji expands on the technical wonders of Jurassic Park and even goes them one better. The wild integration of mammoth animals stampeding through the streets of an erstwhile quiet New England town is a difficult special effect  to convincingly capture on film, yet Johnson’s technical people achieve the effects with startling beauty.

When young Alan Parrish uncovers a long buried mysterious game in a construction site, the powers of magic suck him into a fearsome nightmare that lasts until another young boy finds the board game, Jumanji, in the attic of the former Parrish mansion. When he begins to play the game the dice once again dictate the barrage of special effects until the right numbers bring Alan Parrish back from board game banishment. The now grown up Parrish unites finds his former female flame and with the new resident children of the Parrish house they try to bring order to the chaos caused by the power of Jumanji. There’s plenty of free-wheeling humor provided by devilish monkeys, the cops are confused, the town’s people in panic, but amidst the charging rhinos, screeching bats, and buzz bombing insects, there is never any doubt that order will return to this staid New England town.

This time out Robin Williams as the man-child is flat out terrific, taking the lesson of the misguided Hook and freeing himself from the restriction of aerial nonsense. Playing Alan Parrish coming back twenty-six years after being stuck in a board game, Williams is  restrained in a wild role and gives his character enough edge to tinge the adventure with a coating of fear and horror. Bonnie Hunt is very appealing as the adult object of Parrish’s desire and the children all respond well to the blue screen mayhem..

The sub-plot that runs through Jumanji lacks the straight ahead honesty of the board game aspect of the film and the film falls short here. Fortunately, it’s only a minor nod to fattening the story. Playing out the board game provides enough fun and adventure Jumanji. The device of introducing a hunter from the board game chasing the trophy of Alan’s head provides enough added action to marry with the special effects in creating an effective board game of a movie.

No stranger to special effects or charming adventures, Joe Johnston who directed the very successful Honey I Shrunk the Kids and the less scintillating The Rocketeer,  takes excellent control of Jumanji from the very beginning. The photography captures the vivid dimensions of fantasy and the electronic music of James Horner works very well in the artificial world created on screen. The special effects can be very frightening, so impressionable video pups should be at least supervised to prevent an onslaught of nightmares. If you don’t ask more than a board game diversion from Jumanji, you may find yourself pleasantly surprised.

The video portion of Jumanji is very good. Colors are bright, bursting with energy and mixing with the punch of the film to provide a satisfying look. The soundtrack is outstanding, however, with music and effects surrounding the viewer in this fantasy world.
