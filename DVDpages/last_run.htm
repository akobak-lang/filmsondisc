Last Run/ B-, A-
Universal/2000/113/ANA 1.85

    The Long Run opens with stunning black and white slow motion images of a race run some time complimented by wistful music and dominated by the sound of heavy breathing. It's very effective.
    This is the story of the Comrades Marathon, a grueling 90 kilometer race that captures the imagination of many South Africans.
   The classic structure of training is the discovery, the training, the falling out and then the reunion, The Long Run runs a predicable race.
    There are nice small moments in the film, like Berry's expectant chef hovering over Christine.
    South African countryside is magnificently filmed. There's one scene of cumulous clouds on a blue sky that could be a perfect painting.
   The training sequences are more about building a relationship between the characters. After all, how many times can Berry call out "Faster!" Stahl gives the character of Berry an interesting depth. Earthy performance from       as Berry's daughter.

     Armin Mueller-Stahl has visited South African training territory before in The Power of One, helping to shape a young boxer.

    When Berry gets laid off from his job it also means he is no longer coaching the runners who are to compete in the Comrades Marathon. It's a severe blow. But as luck would have it, Berry discovers another runner, a lonely women refugee who uses running as a connection to her inner self. Berry watches her from a distance, studies her and then convinces her to train under his guidance for the Comrades.

Berry's people skills are questionable at best. It's ironic that a film about long distance runners should have a slightly flabby mid-section. Runners shouldn't have any fat on them. Berry loses his job, his loses runners, his house, his new runner and sure enough in the predicable arc of the film, he and Christine inevitably will return to each other.

    A beautiful transfer! Color and detail are excellent. Effectively punchy image. Interesting dusty look and the brick factor site with dust in the air turning into tightly controlled grain. The clothing is a magnificent explosion of color, tightly controlled throughout. Deep blacks, fine color control. Contrast is beautiful. Very beautiful range. A thoroughly appealing DVD transfer. Every corner of the town is ablaze with color. Nice shadow detail in Berry's study. Dolby 2.0  surround is open and clean with excellent matrixing. The party scene at Berry's is an excellent example of the fine ability of the surround to precisely locate information. Some strange artifacts show up briefly on a black journalist's jacket. Some slight transition edginess with Berry silhouetted against the sky.
