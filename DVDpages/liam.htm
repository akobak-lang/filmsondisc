Liam/ B+,B
Lion's Gate/200/91/ANA 1.85

 

Bearing witness to hate. ©Lion's Gate

   Liam gets a very interesting look. It's almost as if the image has been aged. The lighting is slightly greenish, but it really seems natural to the material.

Shot extremely dark, the shadow detail is very moody. It makes you feel the plight of the characters. Shadow detail is well-balanced.

The father is an extraordinarily proud man, but underneath the pride, waiting like a latent disease, is a detestable vein of hate. The pride he has does give dignity to his family in trying times. Ian Hart is terrific as the father. It's an unsympathetic portrait, but Hart doesn't shy away from the unpleasant aspects of the character.

Teresa is a lovely character, and she's a victim; a victim of intolerance fostered by the Church, a victim of the hateful bile erupting in her father. The film is really about victims and the desperate fight to overcome one's overbearing environment.

Aspects of the treatment of the children by the Church are almost sadistic.

A powerful portrait of pressures of economic depression. Even a man who seems as upstanding as Liam's father succumbs to the hate that has been fostered by upbringing. He's a poisoned man. Man God, think of the prejudices within him, but he's been brought up that way.

You keep waiting for Liam to have a clear articulate outburst. His stutter paralyzes him, but he's a strong boy.

The power of Catholicism over the children is frightening.

There are many touching aspects to Liam. Even when he's down and out, the family rallies round the father. But, when buried bile rises uncontrollably from him, this family that he led with such love, can no longer ignore what they see.

There are a couple of bad splices in the source elements where the film jumped very briefly.
