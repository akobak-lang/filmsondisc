Lonesome Dove/B+,B
Artisan/1989/360m/FS 1.33

     The pacing of Simon Wincer's stunning saga is so thoroughly comfortable that it can be watched by the more determined in one marathon sitting. This epic Western operates on an incredibly personal level for an undertaking of this scope. Watching Lonesome Dove is like joining  its two main characters Gus McCrae and  Captain  Woodrow  Call on their picaresque adventure across the Western country from Texas to Montana.


     The acting is an absolute joy. Robert Duvall invests McCrae with the details of character that bring a screen character to life. Tommy Lee Jones' stoic Call plays in perfect counterpoint with Duvall. Watching these two pros over the hours of this production is like partaking in a feast of thespian inspiration. 
     Wincer again has the great advantage of the marvelous music of Basil Poledouris to help propel the epic cattle drive ever further north. Lonesome Dove manages a miraculous sense of eavesdropping on life.  Even the photography captures a lived in feeling as the images seem coated by a layer of dust that matches the bone dry landscape of much of this unfolding adventure.
     The disc set  looks consistently sharp and the color palette, while often muted, appears to have been what was intended. The pressing is very clean with little grain except in some of the low light scenes. The mono sound is just fine.  A making of documentary is included on the final side and can make for an enjoyable revisit with the characters after experiencing the entire journey.
