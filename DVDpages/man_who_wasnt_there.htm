Man Who Wasn't There, The (SE)/B+, A
USA/2001/116/ANA 1.85

    There's a great, dark edge to the film. Gandolfini is fabulous as Big Dave Brewster. Billy Bob Thornton  is so strange and bizarre. The production design is perfectly elegant and the cinematography is hypnotic. It's as beautiful as a book of still photographs.

The grizzly death scene reminds me a bit of the blocking in Death Simple.

Minimalist piano scores plays perfectly with the action.

The secret to the success of The Man Who Wasn't There is tone. Somehow, the Coen Brothers manage to get such a consistent tone from their actors. These film characters truly seem to inhabit this film noir world.

The homage to Santa Rosa, Hitchcock's setting for the suspensor Shadow of a Doubt, is a gracious not to the master.

The simplicity works very beautifully, but when the plotting becomes more ambitious, moving in various directions, I think the film lost some of its purity.

   Extremely sharp luscious transfer. Black and white has beautiful shadow detail with wonderfully subtle range. The tones are beautifully replicated. This is an absolutely gorgeous transfer. The exquisite lighting is defined with the streaks of lights through the blinds in the barber shop. Go ahead, you can even count the hairs. Nice light output. Check out the sheen on those black cars with night lights reflected.
