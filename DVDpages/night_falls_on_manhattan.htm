Lumet has explored all these themes before. With bits and pieces from his best work cropped together, Night Falls on Manhattan revives some of the vitality somehow missing in much of the late Lumet films. The corruption of Q& A, also written by Lumet, the pure cop of Serpico transformed to a DA,

One of the great strengths of the storytelling in Night Falls on Manhattan is that it doesn’t veer off its chosen road. Working as writer/director has its advantages for Lumet. Writing on familiar ground gives him a clarity of vision as a director.

The romantic sub-plot, while it integrates with the main themes well, never seems to matter much in resolving the film. I can buy the appeal of the lovers for each other, and especially the initial seduction. But I find it lends an extra level of complexity that the film does not need. Another weakness is the melodramatic, almost comic character of Morgy, the legendary District Attorney. As performed by Ron Leibman, he is consistently over the top in every scene. Is that the way the character was written? Probably a little bit of script and actor and director contribute to the flaw.
