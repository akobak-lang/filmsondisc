No Man's Land/B+, B+
DVD Review

No Man's Land begins with a beautifully filmed fog sequence. The lighting is lovely. The transfer is elegant in its fog presentation. It's clear, it flows very naturally with no choppiness. When the fog lifts the rich saturation is a surprise.

This is funny black comedy. Yes, the situation is serious, and sometimes you are forced to laugh at something that shouldn't be funny, but you do laugh. The characters themselves are not funny. The situations and the way the characters interact is funny. It's a very clever comedy that makes strong comment on the war in the Balkans. It's a no win situation. It's kind of cool how they take the war and bring it down to a personal level. Even the petulance and anger of the individuals represent the war and how things can escalate.

Despite the black farcical elements, the devastation of war is the lasting message.

When the fog lifts to reveal the position of the Bosnian replacements in the middle of two front line positions it's a shocking moment.

The situation in No Man's Land is terrifically droll and presents a lot of surprises. The mix of characters is entertaining.

The French sergeant is a strong presence and with his arrival the film's tone shifts. But that's one of the great strengths of No Man's Land, the shifting tones that keep you off balance.

Nice high light output. Fine contrast range. Black are glossy. White English titles are very easy to read. Surround sound is excellent. It recreates the battlefield ambiance very well.
