Novocaine (SE)/ B-, B
DVD Review

     Novocaine plays at a comic tone. There's lots of funny stuff going on, but at the same time it's dark. It's a noir upside-down cake. The cross-cutting is effective and funny. The overwrought music tends to embellish the film with a hyped lurid quality clearly over the top.

 A very off-beat script by David Atkins III matches well with the his direction. It's strange stuff. Black comedy that's hard to hold steady even though the material is consistent and faithful to itself.

It's not quite a satire of film noir, but it does stretch and distort  the conventions. It's almost like a film noir conceived on laughing gas.

    My God, I wonder if there's any appeal to the wired character played by Helena Bonham Carter in her American films. She strung out to dry with little fruit left on the thin branches.

   A bit of graininess in the transfer. The color is excellent. It's quite sharp. Shadow detail is effective. Check out the depth of detail and meticulous separation on Harlan's shirt in the bar scene. There's some high peak transition ringing evident. Black levels are well-handled and shadow detail is in balance. The night scenes are glossy.
