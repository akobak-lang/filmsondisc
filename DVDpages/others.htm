    Strong, sudden nightmare opening is chillingly effective.

Very shadowy atmosphere is salient to the thematic material. The characters live in the shadows, protected from the light. related to central core problem of the children's' light allergy.

The camera moves with horror convention familiarity embellishing the tension. Director of photography Javier Aguirresarobe displays a natural affinity for the genre, in lighting and in camera placement.

The new staff gets the tour. ©Dimension

Alejandro Amenábar serves as his own composer in The Others. It's a terrific score, perfectly tuned to the eerie material. Technically, The Others is a splendid production. All the elements are brought together by Amenabar. Music, lighting, sound effects all serve to enhance the horror experience.

Lots of close-ups promote tension. The faces are lit with the feeling of the great Dutch master painters.

The Others was good enough to practically scare my pants right off. There are a half dozen moments in the film that sent chills down my spine and there was even one full out scream.

Can love and faith overcome the powers of the supernatural? The question floats in the air like a ghostly apparition, but the secrets of The Others

Amenabar maximizes the fear quotient in The Others. Was there even a single false step? I did not detect one.

Kidman almost reminds me of Grace Kelly in this film with her hair swept back elegantly. She is outstanding in The Others. This is her great 2001 performance far outshining her work in Moulin Rouge.

    Very smooth looking transfer, with sharply defined images and no hint of edge enhancement. Even in the many low light compositions textures are easily discerned.  The pallor of the children is perfectly transferred for the desired effect. Delicate colors still retain reasonable saturation in the soft light. Young Nicholas particularly is lit in ghostly fashion. The sound is extraordinarily well-produced. The mysterious moments are enriched by detailed and active surround information. Outstanding dynamic range in the Dolby Digital 5:1 track. An unequivocally reference sonic transfer with marvelous surround directionality and clarity.
     Dimension has packaged The others as a two-disc special edition. Extras include an original twenty-two- minute, documentary, a four-minute visual effects piece, a nine-minute Xeroderma Pigmentosum short, and an eight-minute look at Alejandro Amenabar directing style.
