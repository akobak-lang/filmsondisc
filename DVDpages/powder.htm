DVD Review

Powder

B-, B

US/1995/Color/Widescreen 1.85:1/Stereo Surround, AC-3/112 minutes/Directed by Victor Salva/Starring Sean Patrick Flanery, Mary Steenburgen/Hollywood/16 Chaps/CLV/$39.99

 

Powder is the story of an outsider, who has grown up in almost total isolation because he is albino, presumably from his mother’s being struck by lightening on the day of his premature birth. Brought up by his grandparents, the boy is discovered in their dark basement abode after his grandfather’s death. The police don’t know what to do so a sympathetic social worker played by Mary Steenburgen is brought in to help diffuse any possible situation. What she finds is the incredibly bright young man who calls himself Powder.

Powder is taken to a local home for orphaned boys where he gets the treatment reserved for a shy and different newcomer. It becomes evident that Powder has more going for him than superior intellect as he sets the cafeteria silverware in delightful movement. A science fair toy creates a big charge at the town high school that Powder attends and along the way of misunderstanding he gets to mind meld with a sympathetic girl student.

Robert Patrick Flanery is gentle in the title role, though the white make-up is disconcerting. Steenburgen is likewise fine, but the best performance comes from Lance Henriksen as the town sheriff. Jeff Goldblum is very Jeff Goldblum as the science professor.

Powder is made very well with handsome cinematography from Jerzy Zielinski adding to director Victor Salva’s sharp eye for staging. The simplistic script, also by Salva, is suspect and the lightning-like conclusion is not an acceptable use of deus ex machina. Jerry Goldsmith’s score does not rate with the composer’s best efforts.

The bizarre circumstances surrounding the theatrical release of Powder cannot be ignored in light of the subject matter. Director Victor Salva was convicted of sex related charges stemming from a relationship with an underage boy in one of his earlier film projects. The boy, some five or six years after the offense, showed up at the Hollywood premiere of Powder and picketed the theater, broadcasting Salva's  background and even got into the theater to speak before the Hollywood power audience exposing Salva’s shady past.  It certainly affects the reading of numerous scenes.

Powder is a fine looking disc that shows off its special effects with clarity and distinction. Images are clear, colors alive and contrasts strong. The surround sound adds exciting spatial detail.
