Richard III/A
DVD Review

 

           

Richard III

A, A

UK/1995/Color/Widescreen 2.35:1/Stereo Surround, AC-3/104 minutes/Directed by Richard Loncraine/Starring Ian McKellen, Annette Benning/MGM-UA/29 Chaps/Theatrical Trailer/CLV/$34.98

 

Richard III is a magnificent adaptation of Shakespeare’s historical play dramatizing the rise and fall of the Machiavellian King. Updated from the 15th century to 1930’s Britain, Richard III easily casts allusions to Nazi Germany while working its way through English civil war and the methodical mayhem employed by Richard in his ascension to the throne.

Ian McKellen is mesmerizing in the role of Richard. The language slithers off his lips in natural rhythms. His every movement enhances the performance with added meaning. He brandishes  a smile, small teeth clenched in grin, boldly declaring sin. His stride, his use of single limb, all facets of an actors business are used to full advantage. This magnificent actor invests malevolent Richard with a dark charm born of the delight of pure evil. Once you see Richard III you will never be able to separate the play from McKellen.

Most of the cast is British, but Queen Elizabeth and her brother Rivers are played by Americans Annette Bening and Robert Downey possibly suggesting some hint of  Edward VIII, his abdication and subsequent marriage to American Wallace Simpson. Bening subdues her natural ebullience in the part of Elizabeth, but she fairs okay even in the company of some scene eating British thespians. Downey comes across less well convincingly. Jim Broadbent is very fine as Buckingham, Nigel Hawthorne innocent in the role of Richard’s brother Clarence and Kristin Scott-Thomas positively decadent as Lady Anne, widow of the deposed Prince.

The screenplay by McKellen and director Richard Loncraine is wonderfully economical. It translates the action perfectly, blending the Shakespearean language with astounding success  The striking visual opening sequence leads to the “Now is the winter of our discontent….” speech which opens the play, and is set at the York victory celebration. “My kingdom for a horse,” is too marvelous in context. Costumes and set design are simply seductive in creating  a fascist world of decadence and evil.

What a beautiful laser disc execution. At all times very sharp, the images come alive even in the difficult opening scenes. Black uniforms are deep and foreboding and red banners that set the backdrop for the rallies are bloody garish.  The surround sound is distinct and aggressive. Trevor Jones’ score is delivered in powerful detail. The dialogue is easy to understand. Richard III is one of the great Shakespeare screen adaptations, an unforgettable experience that every film lover should treasure.
