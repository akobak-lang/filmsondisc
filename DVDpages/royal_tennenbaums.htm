Royal Tennenbaums (SE)/ B+, B+
DVD Review

     The Royal Tennenbaums basks gloriously in the eccentricity of its own behavior.

©

    Royal Tennenbaum is one of the great screen rascals. He appreciates his own amoral behavior, dancing through life spreading burrs under those he loves with great glee.

     Some of the consistently funniest bits evolve out of family history. Why is it that Royal constantly reminds Margot that she's adopted? Can Royal be any more obvious about his favoring Richie over Chas? What is it that inspires such behavior by Royal?

     Gene Hackman is at his charming best as Royal. The ensemble acting is terrific in The Royal Tennenbaums. Led by Hackman's charging through life Royal , the family reflects the zany mating of Etheline and Royal. Angelica Huston's reserved Etheline marries slyly with Royal. Gwenneth Paltrow is outstanding as adopted daughter Margot, taking stiffness to the next degree. Ben Stiller, reigned in to a degree, is the perpetually angry Chas Tennenbaum, looking for success with a fierceness directed toward his father. Luke Wilson plays Richie Tennis,  the Balmer, the bearded long-haired former tennis pro with a mournful gaze toward Margot. Owen Wilson is effective as neighbor and would-be Tennenbaum, Eli Cash. Alec Baldwin's narration is beautiful to listen to as he creates an aura around the words, bringing magic to the scene to scene transitions with his sonorous tones.

The plot is very cool. The estranged father of this genius family, coming back into their lives for one last great fling at being a father. What's behind Royal's return? Is it truly his pitiful financial comedown? Or is there more behind the sly smile. Is redemption in the air?
