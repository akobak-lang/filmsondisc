Smoke/B+
DVD Review

 

Smoke

B+, B+

US/1995/Color/Widescreen 1.85:1/Stereo Surround/112 minutes/Directed by Wayne Wang/Starring Harvey Keitel, William Hurt/Miramax/21 Chaps/CLV/$39.99

 

Smoke drifts with delight on the shoulders of its actors and a script that is witty, natural and punctuated by excellent dialogue. Auggie Wren is the manager of a smoke shop in Brooklyn and his life touches varied patrons in different ways. Wren is not the man you’d think to find behind the counter at a small neighborhood store in Brooklyn, but that’s part of the easygoing charm of this film. One of the very interesting conceits of Smoke is Auggie’s religious hobby of taking a photograph each morning of the shop from the same spot which leads to a beautifully realized scene between Auggie and Paul.

Several stories intersect in and around the corner of Auggie’s. Writer Paul Benjamin bumps into Rashid and  Rashid tracks down his long lost father.  Auggie’s old girl friend shows up and takes him to visit another lost soul. The best moments in the film are between Harvey Keitel, brilliantly finding the spark of Auggie and William Hurt as the lonely writer Paul. The two fine actors feed off each others talent creating a beautiful on screen relationship. Wayne Wang directs elegantly from a script written by Paul Auster, the author of the enigmatic The Music of Chance.  The characters in Smoke are driven by cryptic forces of the writer’s imagination.

The ending of Smoke is the most astounding presentation in digest form of one of the film’s major set pieces, Auggie Wren’s Christmas Story.  To the soulful strands of Your Innocent When You Dream, the filmmakers present in stylized black and white images the story of Auggie’s inadvertent Christmas dinner with a blind black grandma with beauteous smile. Tom Waits wrote and sings the song. It’s an awesome ending to the film.

            The professional polish of Smoke is reflected in this excellent laser disc. Colors are very natural, the image is at all times sharp. The music blends wonderfully with the setting and action. Dialogue is perfectly delivered in this fine laser disc presentation. Auggie’s smoke shop is a fine place to spend some laser time.
