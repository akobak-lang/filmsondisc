Story of Qui Ju/A-, B+
DVD Review

 

China/1992Yellow Subtitles/Color/1.33:1 Cropped/Stereo Surround/100 minutes/Directed by Zhang Yimou/Starring Gong Li, Lei Lao Sheng/Col-Tristar/CLV/$34.95

            I love the work of Zhang Yimou. Ask me  to sit down and view any of his films again and you'll  get a quick, unequivocal, delighted assent. More than anything, I think it is Zhang's sense of space and color that is captured in all his films which casts its spell over me. The Story of Qui Ju is thematically very different from Zhang's other works available on laser, Ju Dou and Raise the Red Lantern, both period films that feature passions ignited by the core of Chinese life. They all do, however, share the share exquisite star, Gong Li. But in this latest work of Zhang's the padded presence of Li will surprise you.

            This is a tale set in the provincial villages and cities of  modern China , in which the family and structure have been reshaped by four decades of communist rule. A young pregnant peasant seeks justice for the affront of the village chieftain against her husband. There is no malice is her relentless pursuit of what is right. She is painted in beautiful, muted colors by the very glamorous Gong Li playing brilliantly against type. Qui Ju is forced to take her quest for right up every level of the bureaucratic ladder until she is painfully rewarded by a reality  that subverts the measure of her justice with its ironic timing.

            The plot synopsis may not sound scintillating, but the film is completely engaging; a patchwork quilt  of insightful detail      that adds up to vivid picture of Chinese life. The brilliant signature  photography of Zhang commands the screen frame by frame. The dried red peppers hanging against the eaves of Qui Ju village home are spectacular splashes of color against the bland peasant dress and dull fall/winter landscape. Qui Ju's journeys on her quest for justice are captured by the beautiful long lens of Zhang, while many of the city scenes are recorded by Zhang with the use of hidden cameras.  The details of the city are as fresh to Qui Ju as they are to Western viewers, each stop frame a feast of color and composition.

            If you haven't already had the pleasure of sharing the brilliant work of this consummate filmmaker try visiting the far away world perfectly depicted in The Story of Qui Ju. You'll discover  a heroine  of the first order,  a simple inspiration for the spirit of right.

            The laser disc is both wonderful and disappointing at the same time. The images are sharp and the color controlled yet intense. Maybe the color of the hanging chilies is not quite the same bright red  that reflected off the theater screen, but it is close.  The detail of surround elements is excellent as is the musical score. Slap my wrist if I'm wrong, but the disc appeared cropped to me. My guess it that it was composed 1.66:1.  There are scenes that seem cut off at the edges and the delicately balanced Zhang compositions are compromised on occasion. Mostly though, I was not disturbed by the 1.33:1 aspect ratio. The yellow subtitles were very easy to read and as always a welcome choice. The disc has not  been provided with chapter stops, a oversight that I find dumbfounding. This is likely to be the only chance you get to buy The Story of Qui Ju and despite the questions raised, it is a beautiful experience that will enrich your life.
