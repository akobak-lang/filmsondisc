Tin Men (SE)/ B, B+
Touchstone/1987/108/ ANA 1.85

    Tin Men is often very droll and at other times quite loud and crude. It's about a sub-culture of 1950s Baltimore middle class hustlers in business suits selling aluminum siding,

The feud between BB and Tilly is a funny conceit. The complications may be improbable, but they certainly deliver the laughs and irony.

   Richard Dreyfuss is so comfortable wearing the role of BB. He looks like he is having a hell of a good time.

    The ensemble work of the very talented group of actors makes Tin Men sing. Along with the central stars Dreyfuss and DeVito, Barbara Hershey mouses up very nicely as Nora Tilley, John Mahoney turns in a typically underplayed performance as Moe, Jackie Gayle as Tilley's partner is excellent, with Bruno Kirby as Mouse and Seymour Cassel as Cheese adding tasty acting morsels.

The scams concocted by the Tin Men are truly inventive. They play out with good fun with no ugliness or rancor. 

Levinson adds a string of perfectly representative songs of the era for a sound track. The relationship pairing are well-balanced.

Levinson has a natural feel for dialogue that evolves from characters and situations. It's the heart of the flow of his small films.

The setting link up effortlessly with the first of Barry Levinson's Baltimore films Diner. Aside from scenes played in the same diner, there are also characters that survive from one movie to the next. The costumes are wonderful. They way they dress adds a bright, loud reflection of the characters.

The loud clash of two Tin Men. ©Touchstone

      Tin Men is turned out is a comfortable cinematic looking DVD transfer. It's very sharp with no hint of edginess. Light output is outstanding and the wide contrast range turns out a punchy picture. Depth of image is very nice creating a three dimensional world in which Tin Men plays out. Black levels and shadow detail deliver the package better than any aluminum siding man. Color saturation is right on. Detail is very stable even on the smallest elements. Fabric textures are very life-like. A few scenes are slightly soft likely owing to the focus-pulling on the original.
     The primary element of the special edition is the excellent commentary. It's organized to maximize interest. Actor Bruno Kirby introduces each of the participants as their comments are directed on specific scenes. Director Barry Levinson gets the lion's share of the commentary, along with producer Mark Johnson, costume designer Gloria Gresham, and actors Richard Dreyfuss, John Mahoney, Barbara Hershey, Bruno Kirby and Seymour Cassel. Not every observation is scene specific. Many of the observations are directed about the individuals. Along with the commentary, one expanded scene is included and a theatrical trailer.
