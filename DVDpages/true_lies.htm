True Lies/ C
DVD Review

True Lies, C, A

US/1994/Color/Widescreen 2.35:1/Stereo Surround, AC3/141 minutes/Directed by James Cameron/Starring Arnold Schwarzenegger, Jamie Lee Curtis/Fox/46 Chaps/Theatrical Trailer/2 discs/CLV/THX/$49.98

 

            The technical perfection of True Lies casts such a massive shadow over the frail story that it eclipses most of the potential pleasure. It is a film severely out of balance. It at once wants to hip, hilarious, energetic and still create some semblance of tension. Special effects wizardry may seemingly be able to create something out of nothing, but there is no escaping the fact that without a sound and consistent script what winds up on the screen is a big egg drained of its insides and ready to fracture at the slightest bump.

            True Lies tries hard to be funny, but even when it loses all its confused genre inhibitions, it falls flat with a huge bass bellow. Helen Tasker’s  hotel strip for a knowing husband Harry is supposed to be hilarious, but within the framework of this script, it never comes to life. Lift the scene out into another movie and it just may have been the hilarious moment its creators were hoping for. Much of the film hinges on the fact that Helen doesn’t know Harry’s a secret agent. Even  Lois doesn’t waste as much time on trying to  defrock Clark in the Superman films. An  entire major sub plot that dominates the middle of the film which has Helen being wooed by a Lothario posing as a secret agent is blatantly pathetic and winds up awash in bad taste to boot.

            Even the action scenes, which are often shot spectacularly, have an empty feeling to them. The fact that one sits detached, admiring the special effects, is at once a tribute to the accomplishment and indictment of its pronounced subjugation of the story.

            Arnold Schwarzenegger romped  through The Last Action Hero in better form. With no Terminator skin to protect him under director Cameron’s guidance, Arnold is just another good practitioner of the tango. Jamie Lee Curtis, a fine comedian, may be the best thing about True Lies, but the actress’s virtues cannot overcome the leaden scripting.

            The laser disc is terrific for technophiles at the altar of special effects production. The scenes of the Harrier aircraft nosing into the skyscraper have been reproduced impeccably. The color and resolution is consistently exciting on the disk and the soundtrack powerfully enhances the images. If you want to use True Lies as you demo disc of the moment to show off the bells and whistles of that new home theater, then go ahead and spring for the bucks.
