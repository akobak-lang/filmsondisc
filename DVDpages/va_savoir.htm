Va Savoir/ B+,B+
DVD Review

   A theater company in crisis, a French actress returning to Paris for the first time in three years as an Italian traveling companies leading lady. Stage fright overcomes her   

    Though the film flows with a typically leisurely or casual Rivette pace, but it does not drag. It never fails to hold your interest. Various codas bridge the rhythms of the light airy rhythmic French dance of love and desire. 

     Everybody has eyes for everybody else in this spicy French farce. It plays at a leisurely but comfortable pace without dragging. Character relations, at the heart of the film, are well defined. 

I am not sure if the snippets of the play within the film reveals anything about the character interactions off-stage. 

The finale does play out like the play within the film where all the main characters assemble to sort out the strands of plot, perfectly suited to the farcical tone. 

For a film set in Paris there's almost nothing of the cityscape, but the focus is on the characters, not the city. 

Very interesting variety of women's roles. The three main parts, 

    Each character is groping in the dark to find the right door for their lives. 

Ugo Bassini, the director/lead actor theater company is a charming force. 

Sergio Castellitto is outstanding as Ugo.  Jeanne Balibar is a lithe actress providing Camille with interesting subtext. It's part the way she speaks her lines, part the way she moves, Marianne Basler is a fiery Sonia. Camille is the panther, Dominique a kitten and Sonia a tiger. 

Arthur, Dominique's brother, is a real knuckleball are the otherwise symmetrical playing field. He's not an appealing character by any stretch of the imagination and the sibling relationship leaves a somewhat rancid taste. 

 

You know for certain in a Jacques Rivette film that this is a director who loves women. 

Va Savoir is a very subtle film,, I think the farcical elements grow as the character involvements become more entangled with each other. 

I found it consistently entertaining. I like the interesting group of characters. 

I don't quite understand the everyman appeal of Pierre. What makes these beautiful women so hot for him? 

Putting the pieces together. ©Columbia

    Good light output.  Nice lighting, clean transfer, no edginess, good resolution, shadow detail is fine. Good range of skin tones. Subtle rendering of color. Library is an excellent scene with fine wood tones and delicate lighting replicated accurately. Consistently sharp transfer, easy to look at, it's not a particularly colorful fillm, but that's not the nature of the film.  Removable yellow English subtitles are easy to read, though the translation may not always capture the subtleties of dialogue.
