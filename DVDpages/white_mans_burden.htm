White Man’s Burden

C+, C+

US/1995/Color/Widescreen 1.85:1/Stereo Surround/89 minutes/Directed by Desmond Nakano/Starring John Travolta, Harry Belafonte/HBO/No Chaps/CLV/$39.98

 

No special effects. limited action, and an off-beat story don’t add up to very commercial cinema these days, even when you have the likes of John Travolta on a hot streak starring in the flick. Written and directed by Desmond Nakano, it is White Man’s Burden’s burden to lure an audience to see the film with little ammunition. The film is enjoyable on the simplest level, that is the story and characters play out convincingly and truly do not depend on the film’s major gimmick, role reversal.

The film is walking on ice in essaying this questionable material. It is set in a society where blacks are at the top of the social ladder and whites are abused and ignored. Louis Pinnock loses his job because factory owner Thaddeus Thomas is preoccupied by his own world. When Pinnock is unable to survive he takes matters in a different direction by kidnapping Thomas. Their evolving relationship is the heart of White Man’s Burden and after a bit  it is easy to forget the pedantic lesson offered on the surface.

It’s a pleasure to see Harry Belafonte working and the actor’s graceful presence as Thomas is a nice compliment to the likable Travolta’s Pinnock. Ultimately, this is a difficult film to describe as satisfying.  The events as they unfold do follow through on the logic of the script and the downbeat ending remains true to the film. Bleak filmmaking, low profile, low budget and low audience acceptance is the reality of this film. Still, it’s worth a try.

Lots of the material in this film is dark but it is not unusually grainy. It has a desaturated look but that is the artistic intent. The laser disc is sharp and the sound good, but there is no excuse for the absence of  chapter stops.
