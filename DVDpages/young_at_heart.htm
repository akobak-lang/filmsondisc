Young at Heart/C+,F+
Artisan/1954

     There isn't enough energy to to stimulate an amoeba at the outset of Young at Heart. The comedy kicks in when Gig Young arrives on scene as annoyingly arrogant but winsome composer Alex Burke. Burke quickly wins over the four ladies of the Tuttle household. When Alex arranges to live with the Tuttles while he works at the local Music Institute and composes his latest Broadway offering, romance fills the air. Arranger Barney Sloan adds some mustard to the plain white bread when he arrives to work with Alex. 

Crooning with a smoke. ©Artisan

   There are some charming moments, but Young at Heart is a mixed bag of forced small town sunshine and melancholy dreariness. The script fails to paint a convincing picture of small town life or failed dreams or the complex issues of family relationships. The major relationship doesn't even make sense as portrayed. There's no cohesion to the musical numbers. There's a mixed spirit about the film. It wants to be bright but its overcome with its own melancholy. 
    It's no secret: Doris Day (Laurie) can put over a song. She has a couple of cute numbers on the beach, but Young at Heart turns out more sappy melodrama than upbeat musical. Gordon Douglas' direction is uninspired. Cuts are by the book with annoying reaction shots that serve to sap whatever energy the scenes may have. It's almost thirty-five minutes before Sinatra makes his entrance, far too long. When Barney (Sinatra) meets Laurie, you expects sparks to fly, but the scene actually drags. Sinatra croons a few moody standards, but even the singing talents of Day and Sinatra aren't enough to make Young at Heart solid entertainment.
       The original elements for this transfer of Young at Heart are in miserable condition. The color is faded and the source material is rife with dirt and scratches. Sections of the transfer are distorted from uneven element shrinkage. Image is frequently so as well.  Heavy grain is uneven. It looks like this DVD may have been taken from some inferior composite master source. There's a scratchiness in the soundtrack.  At least the title song sung by Sinatra over the opening and closing credits sounds quite nice. Is the best we can get for starring Frank Sinatra and Doris Day? Sad to say this may be the best for through the tenure of DVD. A DVD presented in such poor condition should have a bold consumer warning on the label indicating the poor element condition.

Reviewed on a Sharp 9000VX DLP Projector
