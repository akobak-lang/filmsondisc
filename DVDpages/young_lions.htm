Young Lions, The/ B, B
Fox/1958/167/ANA 2.35/BW

 

The Young Lions. It’s a pretty big film considering it really deals with personal dramas over the course of the war. The film is slow at times and could have been trimmed effectively. But it’s a good film. I enjoyed watching Brando. His choice of German accent works well, detaching him from the celebrity of actor and disappearing him into the role. He creates a complex sympathetic portrait of a young German doing what he feels is right. On the other side of the Atlantic are Montgomery Clift and Dean Martin as two guys from very different backgrounds, and acting styles too. Clift is often an enigma to me. I admire him and find him off-putting at the same time. Martin is Martin and when the part fits he’s like a well-oiled engine part purring easily. At almost three hours, it’s not unreasonable to expect a little dragging. Joe MacDonald’s widescreen black and white photography is quite beautiful. The transfer is good. The source material has a fair number of scratches and dirt. The DVD goes for sharp over clean and I appreciate that philosophy. Excellent contrast range and shadow detail is perfection. Nicely balanced gray scale.

To Kill a Mockingbird (SE)/ A,A

From the great novel by Harper Lee, this tale of growing up in the 1930s South is splendidly evocative of place and period. Gregory Peck is splendid. Direction impeccable.
