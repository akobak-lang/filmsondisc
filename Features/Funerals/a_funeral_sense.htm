A Funeral Sense

    Funerals are often pivotal sequences in films. The style of a funeral often tells much about the filmmakers.

One of the best funeral sequences introduces several important characters in Stanley Donen's Charade. It combines the elements of Charade to perfection, capturing the menace of the characters while emphasizing the unique combination of humor. Audrey Hepburn sits demurely in the front role of the funeral parlor with a friend, her porcelain face scrimmed through a a broadly meshed balck veil. She is a picture of elegance in black.  The inspector investigating the circumstances surrounding the death of Charles sits on the sidelines clipping his nails. Donen frames a shot with coffin in the foreground looking back toward the empty room as Hepburn notes with rye delivery, "Not a very large turnout, is it."
