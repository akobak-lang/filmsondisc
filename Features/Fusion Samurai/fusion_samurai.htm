Fusion Samurai

By Stu Kobak

      Wandering down the main street of a deserted town, the gruff looking samurai, has all senses on alert. A mosquito crossing his path is scrutinized as a potential threat. A role of his shoulders brings a distinctive physical characteristic to the samurai that will bring a familiar smile later.

     The first successful fusing of Samurai and Western films to my recollection is Sidney Pollack's The Yakuza.

     The Challenge brings a westerner into the culture of the samurai warrior. Scott Glen is a wonderful pupil for the sage master Toshiro Mifune.

    Black Rain is a terrific blending of samurai, cop film, gang epic and so forth. Michael Douglas bulldogs his way into the underworld of Osaka under the guidance of Ridley Scott.

    A typical Hitchcock ploy sets the action in motion in director             The Hunted. Christopher Lambert is a businessman.........

     Taking a samurai out of his element is yet another fusion samurai dialogue. Toshiro Mifune is the perfect candidate to initiate an entry of the samurai into a western film, and with Red Sun, elements of the samurai code are effectively relocated to the great American west. Too bad the film fall flat in too may other ways.
