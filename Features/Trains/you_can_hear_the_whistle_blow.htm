You Can Hear the Whistle Blow

The train as a central image or a dominate image in a film has been historically frequent image in cinema. The French Connection has two marvelous train sequences, one involving the doors and Charnier's iluding the tail of Popeye Doyle and then there is the marvelous car chasing the train sequence that culminates on the elevated staircase by Doyle shooting Charnier's henchman.

            Strangers on a Train focuses on the anononmity of train travel and while the centrail image of the train appears in the title, the unraveling of the plot after the inital meeting on the train does not require tjhe  presence of the train.

            The Train is a film that is totally dominated by the rails and smokestack of its engine. Runaway Train is another film that that realizes the majority of its mise en scene aboard a train.

            The Lady Vanishes is one of the most familiar of train pictures. The mechanics of the train are not that important, nevertheless, much pof the action takes place on a train. Similarly, Night Train to Munich, a film admittedly taking advantage of the success of Hitchcock;s earlirt film, spends an extraordinary amount of time on the train.

            The train crash in The Fugitive is considered one of that film's major set pieces and although it takes place in the course of only a few momemts, the image of the train and bus colliding is a very powerful train statement. Even in Schindler's List, trains play a very important part, as they do in almost all holocost films.
