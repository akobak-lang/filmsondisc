DVD: Ten Reasons to Jump Right In.

By Stu Kobak

Many videophiles are waiting, sitting on the fence like a phalanx of black crows, watching the writhing body of the laser disc format struggle for survival. Yet, they are still not crossing over to DVD. Here are ten reasons why you should cross the fence onto the DVD side of video.

  1. DVD is clearing the best consumer video software available today. It produces pictures superior to laser disc.

  2. The virtual absence of video and color noise on DVD.

  3. Most feature films fit comfortably on a DVD with no side changes and with the implementation of RSDL dual layer usage, virtually any length movie can be viewed with no interruption.

  4. Anamorphic delivery of images on many DVDs is an enormous advantage over regular letterboxing. An anamorphic image is presented in a squeezed format and then unsqueezed by a video display device. When viewed on a widescreen ready tube or projector, loss of vertical resolution is minimized and the resultant pictures are often stunning.

  5. DVDs are not sensitive to warping like laser discs. Many lasers when not perfectly stored warp slightly and the result is unsightly cross-talk artifacts in the picture. I have yet to see a DVD exhibit this annoying image compromise

  6. DVD is a component video delivery system which needs no NTSC decoding. on display devices that accept component input, the inherent many of the inherent artifacts of decoded NTSC video are eliminated or minimized.

  7. The Wild Bunch is an astounding bargain on DVD compared to the price of the Warner Special Edition laser disc. At $24.95, it has the same transfer as the $124.95 laser disc and both presentations include the fine documentary on the making of the film. Yes, the laser box includes a soundtrack CD and a book, but you can search high and low and won't find a Ben Franklin's there. Take the hundred bucks saved and plunk it down toward a new DVD player.

  8. There is a clear price advantage over laser. Typical list price is $24.95, while laser's typically retail from $34.95 to $39.95. More than a few of these bargain priced DVDs include second audio commentary usually included only expensive laser special editions. Check out Thinner from Republic or Demolition Man from Warner or Shine from New Line.

  9. DVD releases present an opportunity to revisit some great movies.

  10. Fence sitters site the coming of the HDTV format as a reason for DVD hesitation. Remember, HDTV display devices should have the capability of unsqueezing anamorphic images, so even if you don't have a widescreen television today, when you change to the next generation of TV, your anamorphic DVDs will still look great.

Contrarians could probably string an impressive list of reasons for not buying a DVD player. It's not a perfect format and the vagaries of the market place has placed limits on the confusion threshold of the consumer. Packaging varieties are a nightmare, questionable transfers of public domain titles are flooding the market place, and so forth. But the fact remains, DVD is the best video incarnation we have yet to experience and in coming months the avalanche of movies will continue to flow down from the mountaintops of the studios, especially if more consumers get on board.
