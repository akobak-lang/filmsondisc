Here’s Looking at You, Kid
By Stu Kobak

      Here’s looking at you kid. There’s a line everyone recognizes. The key word is kid. Hollywood and the kid. Don’t underestimate the power of a three-letter word. Kid is shorthand for so many feelings. It’s a term of respect; it’s a term of affection. Thin about all those characters who were called kid, or The Kid.

     Sometimes, kid was used in it’s most literal sense, like in The Kid, the great Chaplin film, remade with Wallace Beery as a talkie. Kid was magic for Beery. The great bear of a gravelly voiced actor walked away with an Academy Award for his portrayal of drunken prize fighter trying to touch his kid.

     Kid Shelleen is one of the funniest western characters ever portrayed on the screen. Lee Marvin won the Academy Award for his performance as the former gunfighter turned drunk. Kid, like in Billy the Kid, was often a term of respect in vintage Westerns, so when Cat Ballou came around, the term was ripe for kidding. Everyone associated with Cat Ballou makes the most out of Marvin's outrageous portrayal and it will forever mark a special stamp on the history of "kid" in the movies.

      In more serious Westerns, Billy the Kid has been portrayed by a variety of talented stars. Robert Taylor, MGM's romantic leading man of the thirties provided Billy the Kid with an elegance not often seen in kid roles. The 1941 
    Director Sam Peckinpah took his best shot at the kid in Pat Garrett and Billy the Kid. Kris Kristofferson looks like he never was a kid, but in Peckinpah's Western he carries the guns fast and loose while trying to evade the inevitable showdown with Pat Garrett. 
    Paul Newman was a terrific choice as Billy the Kid in Arthur Penn's lean 1958 black and white Western. Newman, always a good choice to produce sympathy from the darker side of his characters, knew he was on to something good with the kid. He later played Butch to the Sundance Kid in the easy to like Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid  in 1969 and knew more of good thing when he taught kid Johnny Hooker everything he knew about the con in The Sting.
