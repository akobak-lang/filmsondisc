8-Bit Laser Page 3  

Film on Disc has 2-bit, 8-bit and 16-bit reviews. 2-bit reviews include letter ratings only. 8-bit reviews are brief reviews including DVD quality assessment and 16-bit reviews are full length. This is the 8-Bit Review Page. DVD ratings are two part, the first letter representing film content, the second letter for film element , transfer and pressing quality.

Caught/A-,B+

Sony Classics/1996/109m

     A fish, swimming innocently in the sea, caught by a net is the image that languorously slips onto the screen as the end credits role. Caught is an updating of The Postman Always Rings Twice with the locale effectively shifted to Brooklyn.
     The players are fascinating to watch. This is the best of Edward James Olmos. The fire in his stare, the joy of life in his laugh, the hurt in his eyes, capture all the parts of Joe Diaz. It’s a beautifully modulated performance. Maria Conchita Alonzo, who filmed Caught while starring in Kiss of the Spider Women on Broadway, is a ravishing Betty. Longing for another start in life, she latches onto Nick, a young drifter who wanders into the fish store exhausted and in need of help. The Diaz’s take Nick in, put him to work in the fish store and board him in their grown son’s vacant room. But as the weeks swiftly pass, the close confines of the small apartment light fires of desire under Betty and Nick. Caught is a sensual and mature work of film art.

Good vintage. ãDisney

     The naturalistic colors of director Robert Young are captured effortlessly on this laser disc. Images are as clearly focused as the noiresque tale. The haunting jazz score is beautifully modulated. Surround information is minimal, but the only explosions are the fiery emotions of the actors.

Michael/C+,A

Warner/1996/106m

     Michael lays itself at the wings of John Travolta’s charm. Travolta plays horny, grubby Archangel Michael in his last big Earth job(It seems angels have a limited number of Earth opportunities.). Travolta ruffles those feathers, puffs up his chest, and appears to have a real good time playing matchmaker to William Hurt and Andie MacDowell. The featherweight script is too heavy for an Archangel to carry.
     Hurt is well cast as Frank Quinlin, cynical reporter for the National Mirror, a prototypical supermarket tantalizer. Andie MacDowell plays Dorothy Winters, a dog trainer and would-be country singer, hired by the editor of the Mirror to catch Quinlin in a false step. Bob Hoskins appears in a forgettable role as the Mirror’s hungry editor.

Good vintage. ãDisney

     Directed by Nora Ephron and written by a quartet of scribes, including herself and sister Delia Ephron, Michael’s journey from the farmlands to the bright lights of Chicago is bland. The depiction of Michael as pure blue collar is a departure from traditional angel screen portraits, but the originality is married with an unsavory coarseness.
     Michael is a stunning laser disc. It’s balances delicate lighting with enough strong contrasts to make a consistently appealing presentation. Image and color are outstanding. The music by Randy Newman has excellent depth.

Blood and Wine/B,B

Fox/1997/101m

     Blood and Wine, the image is stark, enticing, dark and brooding and for the first half of this movie lives up to those expectations. Jack Nicholson has one of his best roles in years as the double dealing wine merchant Alex Gates and Michael Caine has a great role as sleazy crook Victor Spansky. The chemistry between these two actors is terrific. Along for the trip are Stephen Dorff, Jennifer Lopez and Judy Davis, all turning in good performances. Unfortunately, the intricacy of the set-up is followed by what is mostly a bizarre chase film. Still, it's worth a watch, if only to see these actors are an interesting playing field.

Good vintage. ãDisney

       Rafelson’s direction is crisp. Blood and Wine has a terrific look. The camera work of Newton Thomas Sigal(Usual Suspects)provides a perfect view of Miami and environs.
     Images are not consistently sharp in Blood and Wine. Increased shadow detail would have served the laser disc in better stead. The dark world of noir requires shadowy images and they rarely make for easy transfers. Color is good.

Fierce Creatures/B, A-

Universal/1997/94m

     You could say that Fierce Creatures ultimately has teeth bigger than its bite. An entertaining concoction, its strains for originality with irreverence, failing to wholly succeed on either front. When its does tickle the funny bone, usually during its silliest moments, Fierce Creatures is a perfect Sunday at the zoo. Trying to capitalize on the magic of the star chemistry from A Fish Called Wanda, Fierce Creatures brings those stars together again with decidedly less success.

Ape attractions. ãUniversal

     Will the zoo have to put its friendly animals to sleep in favor of a more hostile and supposedly commercially appealing environment. Will Rollo Lee prove ruthless enough as zoo manager. Who gets to sleep with Willa. Does lothario Vince prove himself to his father. These are the questions that Fierce Creatures tries to spear with abandon.
      John Cleese stars as Rollo Lee the executive chosen to take over a London Zoo and make it profitable. Cleese is the perfect choice to make all the tough decisions in his inimitable screen fashion. Kevin Kline plays the dual role of media magnate Rod McCain and his son Vince. Kline is a delightful comedian and makes the most of his screen time. Jamie Lee Curtis rounds out the stars with a forward performance as hungry executive Willa Weston. The madness often works on several counts and the romantic entanglements are lots of fun.
      Fierce Creatures is fine transfer to laser disc. A bright looking transfer, the disc has well defined color. Hues are accurate. The image is consistently sharp. Creature sounds are well located.
