A Western is a Western is a Western                                Page 4

My Darling Clementine/A, B+

 
US/1946/BW/Fullscreen/Mono/117/Directed by John Ford/Starring Henry Fonda, Victor Mature/Fox Video/6 Chapters/CLV/$39.98 
 
This is truly one of the great Western masterpieces. It is the  leanest and best rendering of the legendary Earp shoot-out at the O.K. Corral.  John Ford weaves his supreme compositions into an elegant story simple told. It is a straight line of perfection from beginning to end. 
 
My Darling Clementine is graced by  Henry Fonda as Wyatt Earp, a laconic screen portrait that is representative of the best work Fonda has ever crafted. Fonda brings to Earp an element of truth and integrity rarely  depicted on the screen with such convincing conviction. Under Ford's terse direction, you believe every syllable Fonda utters. Victor Mature co-stars opposite Fonda as Doc Holliday and Mature, though known more for his physique than thespian ability, reaches beyond any limitations of skill in capturing a complex Doc Holliday. The array of character actors that always shine under Ford's eye are a joy to behold.  Slithering through his role like a dust covered rattle snake, Walter Brennan sears his brand on Old Man Clanton. The strength of the smaller roles brings a greater life to the whole. 

 The photography of Joseph P. MacDonald is stunning. High key lighting techniques create shadows and highlights that emphasize the power of Ford's screen. The Western takes on the veneer of film noir in My Darling Clementine, bending and shaping it beautifully to laminate over the contours of the genre.  
 
My Darling Clementine demands very little of the viewer except to sit back and watch these consummate professionals  work together in  unusual symphonic harmony. Enjoying a Western achievement such as this is almost obligatory in terms of evaluating the  genre. Compare Ford's story of Wyatt Earp with the flabby and unfocused contemporary treatment of this season's Tombstone.  Even compare it to Gunfight at the O.K. Corral, a very good film, and you begin to understand the level of genius involved in My Darling Clementine. 

 This laser disc has been in the catalogue for a number of years. I'd like to think it's still available due to numerous repressings, but sadly, my instinct suggests that not every disc of the initial pressing has   wisely taken up residence in laser disc collections.  The luminous photography has been transferred from a wonderful print. All the details of lighting that raise the level of the film are rendered perfectly. The image is very sharp and there is little indication that the film is almost fifty years old. The mono sound is fine. The pressing had no dropouts, nor did it suffer from a graininess too often evident in older discs. It would be nice to have additional chapter stops available to jump to favorite moments, but other than that the disc is right up to date.
