Box of Moonlight/C,B-

Trilmark/1996/112m

     After seeing the wonderful Living in Oblivion, I had high expectations for Box of Moonlight. You might even say I was predisposed to liking it. Try as I might and try as director Tom DiCillo might, I was not able to warm to this comedy that struggles futilely for laughs and life observations.
     John Tutturro is faced with a life crisis. While on road assignment as a construction foreman, as the project is suddenly cancelled, he realizes that none of the workers like him, that he has lost the ability to enjoy himself. Realizing that he isn't too far from a lakeside resort he remembers from his childhood, he decides to return to that place of innocence to reassess his life. He finds the lake, but it's a memory poluted by time and chemicals.
     Everything changes, however, when John stops to help Jim on the road. Jim is the proverbial free spirit, a creature existing on the fringes searching for the pure joy of life. He initiates John to a taste of this freedom and anarchy and while John resists with the cold arsenal of practicality built . Jim seduces a pair a small town girls and John is eventually swept into the experience.

Frolicsome foursome.©Trimark
