Mr. Holland’s Opus A-, A-

US/1995/Color/Widescreen 2.35:1/Stereo Surround, AC-3/143 minutes/Directed by Stephen Herek/Starring Richard Dreyfuss, Glenne Headly/Hollywood/24 Chaps/CLV/2 discs/$

The episodic structure of movies covering ambitious time spans is a high hurdle to leap for success. Mr. Holland’s Opus is a high jump champ. It effortlessly chronicles the forty years of Glen Holland's life as he reluctantly grows into the job of a high school music teacher.

Though much of the praise for this film has focused on the fine Dreyfuss performance, director Stephen Herek's sense of pacing is dynamite. The almost two and a half hour film never drags. Each segment of Holland's life moves on before the sharpness of the time dulls which leads to the blatantly sentimental ending which could have been lifted right out of Frank Capra's It's a Wonderful. Wait a second, did I say that. It's okay, because even if it is a reworking, updating, fresh look, what have you, of those themes, Mr. Holland’s Opus is a beautiful and fulfilling film experience.

Richard Dreyfuss plays Holland beautifully. Dreyfuss restrains his often overzealous screen energy to craft a flesh and blood human being of writer Sean Patrick Duncan's's title character. The actor reaches deep into himself for this role. Holland seduces his students bring music to them through any channel of understanding available. Dreyfuss also seduces his audience. We care about Holland, just as Holland cares for his students. It's a rich, rewarding performance.

Director Herek works very well with all his actors in support of Dreyfuss great work. Glenne Headley is an understanding wife and Jay Thomas lends a warmth to the role of athletic coach Bill Meister. Jean Louisa Kelly is a beautiful screen presence as Rowena, who represents Holland's unfulfilled dreams.

Herek conducts the various elements of Mr. Holland’s Opus with a veteran screen man's knowing touch though he still has some water behind his ears. The cinematography by Oliver Wood is lively and upbeat. Michael Kamen's score is appropriately sentimental and successfully uses elements from the film's structure. David Nichols makes Holland's thirty years perfectly in synch with the times through excellent production design.

This is a stunning laser disc. It been transferred cleanly from perfect elements with bright levels and dynamic contrasts. Colors are intense, fully saturated, blacks strong. The night scenes are lovely to look at without grain. Perhaps the sharpness has been tweaked too much, but in most instances in works beneficially. Watch out for the school's staircase though. Music is a major part of Mr. Holland’s Opus and has been recorded on this laser disc with a beautiful full dynamic range. The lush orchestration and composition of Michael Kamen is served professionally.
