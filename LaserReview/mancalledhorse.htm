A Man Called Horse/B+, B+ 


US/1970/Color/Widescreen 2.28:1/Mono/114 minutes/Directed by Elliot Silverstein/Starring Richard Harris, Judith Anderson/Fox Video/20 Chaps/CLV/$49.98 

 Well filmed attempt at capturing the raw sense of the savage experience through Western eyes. Englishman Lord John Morgan is imbibing the American frontier through a private hunting expedition when his sudden capture by hostile Indians changes the course of his life. 

 Richard Harris makes the most of this plum role, groveling in the dirt and comfortably cavorting bare bottomed whenever necessary(Can you imagine, he's going nude again almost 25 years later in Wrestling with Ernest Hemingway, currently showing on theatrical screens.). Harris is often painfully convincing and has wonderful support from Dame Judith Anderson, who makes the most of her moments as Harris's initial tormentor.  

 Elliot Silverstein uses the wide screen vistas to grand effect. The director moves the film along at a crisp pace despite its almost two hour running time. The action sequences are dynamic and he maintains an authentic feel to the film. 
 
This atypical  Western offers up some entertaining viewing moments. It sits comfortably with some of its more traditional genre brethren. 
 
The disc is an excellent production. It has been transferred from a first class print and the image is sharp and colors accurate. The monaural sound is especially fine. Some of the difficult interior scenes fare very well in this pressing.
