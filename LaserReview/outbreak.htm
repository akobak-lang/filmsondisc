OUTBREAK/C+, A

US/1995/COLOR/WIDESCREEN 1.85:1/STEREO SURROUND, AC-3/128 MINUTES/DIRECTED BY WOLFGANG PETERSEN/STARRING DUSTIN HOFFMAN, RENE RUSSO/WARNER/45 CHAPS/2 DISCS/CLV-CAV/439.98

I remember going to the theater to see Outbreak with enormous anticipation expecting another triumph from director Wolfgang Petersen after the enormous success of his polished In the Line of Fire. Alas, though packed with all the ingredients of a first rate thriller, the screenplay and execution ultimately lumped together in an uncontrolled mess. When I put the disc on I immediately became involved in the film. The images on laser were thrilled and marvelously executed. Was this the same film I’d seen? Was it just that I’d had a bad day? I opened up to the film and went with the flow. Sure enough, by the time Petersen decided to follow the path of the runaway virus with a slow motion photography, Outbreak began to self-destruct. The all important monitor of reality had been irretrievably lost.

You could argue that there are other elements of Outbreak that attacked its healthy premise. Dustin Hoffman is not the first actor you think of when it comes to casting a thriller, but I think Hoffman does just fine. It’s the script that lets him down. The screen chemistry between Hoffman and co-star Rene Russo is also questionable. Morgan Freeman is too reluctant a villain and Donald Sutherland cut too much in the mold of pure comic book. The screenplay could have focused more successfully on combating the epidemic rather than veering off in the direction of a cover-up plot.

You can’t fault the production values of the film. It’s filmed handsomely by Michael Ballhaus and production design by William Sandell is convincing. Petersen’s choice of pacing and editing is suspect ,however, and the unsatisfactory deliver of script is insurmountable.

The disc production is another matter altogether. Outbreak is a knock-out of a disc. The images are as sharp as can be. Colors are accurate and the transfer is very bright with excellent blacks. Explosive transitions look spectacular on the screen. There is little in the way of visual artifacts on the disc. The sound is awesome. From the whirring rumble of a helicopter to the nuking of a contaminated area, Outbreak delivers top bang for the buck.
