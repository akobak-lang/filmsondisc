PANTHER/B-, D

US/1995/COLOR/WIDESCREEN 1.85:1/STEREO SURROUND/120 MINUTES/DIRECTED BY MARIO VAN PEEBLES/STARRING KADEEM HARDISON, BOKEEM WOODBINE/POLYGRAM/CLV/$34.95

Panther is a film with a slick interface, to borrow from computer terminology. It covers an explosive time of recent history from the perspective of The Black Panther Party. No doubt there is a great deal of truth to both sides of any historical argument, and it is the director and writer’s prerogative to present the best case for their view of history as convincingly as possible. Panther does a good job of presenting the Panthers as victims of a FBI Hoover driven plot. It certainly wouldn't surprise me if it were true.

Panther is strongest when it attempts to capture the period in faux documentary style. The personal story that has been woven into the history to provide a dramatic structure is clearly the least interesting. I would rather have spent more screen time with Newton and Seale rather than the fictional character Judge, narrator, witness, and victim.

Marcus Chong is excellent as Huey Newton. He conveys the passion of an idealist combined with the fire of a realist. Courtney B. Vance holds his own as Bobby Seale, but Kadeem Hardison seems less real and Joe Don Baker as the cop on Judge’s case, a cardboard cracker in a Frisco locale.

This is Mario Van Peebles’ second outing as a feature film director and he displays a strong command of this project. The screenplay was written by his father, Melvin Van Peebles, and just as the senior Van Peebles builds his script with straight ahead confidence, Mario directs with equal comfort and to better effect.

There is one unforgivable aspect to this laser disc transfer: it appears that the film has a slight jitter during the transfer process and that has translated into exacerbated artifacts for the laser disc. Otherwise, the disc has very sharp images and excellent screen pop. Blacks are deep and there is almost no evident grain. The many night scenes are transferred in excellent fashion. The soundtrack equals the punch of the picture. The shoot-outs are sonically dramatic. Too bad about that jitter.
