SPECIES/C, B+

US/1995/COLOR/WIDESCREEN 2.35:1/STEREO SURROUND, AC-3/108 MINUTES/DIRECTED BY ROGER DONALDSON/STARRING NATASHA HENSTRIDGE, BEN KINGSLEY/MGM/38 CHAPS/THEATRICAL TRAILER/CLV/THX/$3498

Species could have been called "The Fantastic Five," as the most ridiculous group of alien hunters ever assembled try to track down the beautiful Natasha Henstridge, whose great looks go a lot further than any story telling to achieve some level of satisfaction from this stranger in a strange land tale gone astray. Species begins on a promising note as scientists in a government laboratory are about to execute a little girl, who, it turns out, has been born by combining human and alien DNA. Well, she’s growing too fast so it’s time to get rid of her. Why in heaven’s name the gas chamber though? Well, Ben Kingsley playing Fitch, the head of the project, sheds a tear over the impending execution. Right. It hasn’t run down the length of his face before the girl creature has kicked her way out of the glass chamber and the chase is on.

Fitch recruits a group to find the alien: Michael Masden is a government assassin, Alfred Molina an anthropologist, Forrest Whitaker a psychic and Marg Helgenberger a biologist, the perfect team to track Sil, as she is dubbed by Fitch. Before you know it, Sil has been reborn as a gorgeous 21-year-old desperately looking for a mate. It’s the human way to propagate, you know. She manages to kill and kiss a few people in her frantic search for the perfect mate. Will these five crack trackers get to Sil before she consummates. Get her a break guys, she’s only a growing girl.

The climatic confrontation winds up below ground in the sewers where Sil has morphed into a pregnant Gigeresque creature a la Aliens. Sounds like the right locale. Species is really pretty bad, bad enough to be enjoyable. Director Roger Donaldson moves the action a brisk pace, but this is a script born of suspect DNA.

Species does look and sound great. The transfer is as immaculate as Sil’s conception. Much of the film is set in dark locales and the detail is easily discernible. Contrast levels could have had additional range, but the blacks are deep and there is minimum grain in all the dark scenes. The sound is excellent. The delightful little sonic details of Sil’s transformation are sure seduce film lovers. Don’t be surprised to see a sequel created from alien DNA around the multiplex corner. How about Species II: The Birth of a New Nation.
