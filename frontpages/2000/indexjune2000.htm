Movie Mags on the Net
Movie magazine have made a full-scale invasion of the Net and online users are getting big benefits. Boutique publications are competing with the big boys presenting a wealth of movie information. 

Home Theater Talk is giving away a subwoofer and outboard amp Sunday at high noon. Home Theater Talk is guided by the inimitable style of enthusiast Mike Knapp. Friendly atmosphere and knowledgeable folks are the secret ingredients.


Doug Pratt's been doing it longer than just about anyone in the business, reviewing laser discs, that is, and now DVDs too. The venerable Laserdisc Newsletter has been publishing since 1984. Pratt probably has a large enough stock of laser discs in his house to build a DVD-proof bomb shelter. Check out his on-line reviews at DVDlaser.com.

Avid-Collector Posters

Great selection of big foreign paper, plus the home of J. Fields linen service.

DVDcorner.net is a friendly netsurfer stopover with lots of DVD reviews and DVD news. Stop by the site for a chance to win a DVD of The Bone Collector.


The Cinema Laser announced a milestone of 500 DVD reviews online.
The home grown print magazine originally for laserphiles has been publishing for a number of years and has embraced DVD in a big way. Lots of helpful information.


DVD Verdict now features more than 500 DVD reviews from an enthusiastic staff of movie lovers. Chief Judge Sean McGinnis and right and left hand Kathy Leach wield the Verdict's magic wand.

Choko Movie Posters
The  popular Paris movie poster store makes it's way to the Internet with an outstanding selection of French Movie Posters.  Many beautiful images on display in a searchable data base.

Gary Morris's insightful publication Bright Lights Film Journal turns the celluloid in films from a unique perspective. Click on the image above for more pure movie views.


Home Vision Cinema
 Classic and foreign motion pictures on VHS tape. The title selection is excellent and informative essays are provided for many  of the films.

Imaging Science Foundation
The Imaging Science Foundation promotes proper standards in home theater viewing. ISF trained technicians offer monitor calibration services to consumers. The difference in a  properly calibrated monitor can be astounding. Click on the image to find an ISF member near you.
