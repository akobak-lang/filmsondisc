 Copper Bowl Copper Skies

January 1, 2000

       Happy New Year everyone! January 1st was a lovely day here. This is the first day of the Copper Bowl Tournament. Happily, with an 8 am match, we were lucky that the weather was somewhat milder than it has been. Liz played at the La Ventana site, a beautiful resort in the mountains. It was a short match, 6-0, 6-1 against a player from Illinois, Maggie Caulk. Not even a work-out unfortunately.

Desert Girl at home in the Desert Museum

     The early morning match gave us an opportunity to do some touring. We took a forty minute drive into the mountains to The Desert Museum in Saguaro National Park. Winding up the mountain roads brought us closer and closer to those little sticks on the mountains which were hundreds upon hundreds of cacti. Thu running joke was that the girls could not find a cactus thicker than Dad. Ho, ho, ho.

Chiseling for artifacts.

     The museum was a multifaceted little gem. The desert gardens were a treat as we walked the paths of the museum. We descended into a dark cave a got a charming view of life in the mineral world. There was the smallest zoo of local animals in t r natural environment. The puma, black bear, otters, beaver and deer. The birds were lots of fun. I help a very long conversation with a road runner. My lips are still sore from puckering up.
    The real treat of the museum was a pair of aviaries. One was filled with local birds enjoying the greenery. The second was strictly for hummingbirds. There were only 17 in the aviary at the time. They don't get many more than 30 at any time since these tiny birds are highly territorial and anxiety is extremely hazardous to their health. It was a delightful time for the three of us.

Hummingbird at rest.

The elegant entrance to La Ventana

     We arrived back in the Tucson area in time for Lizzie to do some light drilling with her friend Juliana. Afterward, we headed out for a spectacular dinner at La Ventana. We were far too underdressed to be respectable, but we were seated in a pleasant alcove. The hotel is truly a five star resort and the restaurant proved itself over every course with a bonus of a world class wine list. This may be the only time that I ask a captain to add water to an ice bucket that Evian will be used. That provided some good laughs. A nice Louis Roederer champagne was followed by a wonder 1993 Pommard from Hubert de Montille. Yum. Four of the five of us ordered Dover Sole in a scrumptious preparation. The lobster salad appetizer was wrapped in a closed flower of tomatoes that opened gloriously at a slight touch forming perfect pedals around the lobster and chanterelles. The sky was filled with twinkling stars as we departed the restaurant and headed back for some needed rest.
