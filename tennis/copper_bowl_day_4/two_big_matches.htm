Two Big Matches

January 4,  2000

     Liz ran into the Lindsay Burdette Express in her quarterfinal Copper Bowl match yesterday morning at the El Conquistador Country Club. Burdette, who won the Super National 12s hard courts earlier this year, is an aggressive player from Georgia. She goes after the ball big time, taking it on the rise and taking high balls out the air. She often wails at the ball, and when they are going in, watch out brother. Liz couldn't get her feet moving early enough and Burdette blasted her off the court in the first set, 6-0. As they say, it takes two to make a tennis match; in this case a combination of Burdette's aggressive play and Lizzie's static feet. Down 2-0 in the second set, Liz began battling back, displaying the guts and shot making that got her to the quarters. The points were contested with ferocity and as Liz pounded them at Burdette, she forced errors. Still. Burdette went out to a 5-2 lead. Liz did not give up, but turned on the pressure, hitting lots of winners. Serving a 5-6, after a long game, she forced a tie-break. Too bad, but Burdette prevailed 7-4 in the tie-break. The loss put Liz into the quarterfinal play-offs with her first match scheduled for later in the afternoon at Country Club again.

       After losing earlier to Burdette in the Quarterfinals,  playoffs for fifth place at the Copper Bowl. Liz took the court at 3:30 pm at Country Club for the playoffs for fifth place against Emmy Fritz-Krockow of Virginia. Emmy won the 12s Copper Bowl last year and is a hard hitter with a very fluid game. Liz came out banging, looking like the Queen of the court in the first set. Her powerful strokes won her a lot of points, but Fritz-Krockow went for a lot on many of Lizzie's serves, blasting numerous winners. Liz prevailed 6-3. The second set looked like it would be more of the same, but deep into the set, at 4-2, Liz, she became a little tentative and Emmy started cracking more winners off some short balls. There a couple of breathless exchanges at the net and Emmy came from behind to win the set 6-4. Another three set match: just what we didn't want. Again in the third set Liz came out strong and confident. Leading 4-2, she once again became too conservative and lofted a few short balls into the court and before you knew it, Emmy was back. Liz took the lead to 5-3, Emmy battled back to 4-5, and while we held our collective breath on the sidelines, Liz took the match with some strong ground strokes 6-4 in the third. The victory put her in the finals for fifth place against Alexis Plukas, who she had beaten in three sets at the National 14s last month. She plays in the morning at Country Club bright and early at 9. Plukas is waiting and hungry.

A Janos after dinner smile.
       Our dinner celebration took us to the elegant Westin La Paloma Resort, home of Janos, one of Tucson's finest restaurants. Liz marveled at a gold leaf ceiling and expressed a wish for a ceiling like that at home. Eat your food girl!
    Truly a major league wine list at Janos to go with the Southwest fusion cuisine. After spending my usual too much time perusing the selection, I decided we would try a 1995 Valbuena from Vega Sicilia, the great house of Spain. This bottle was a mini wine lesson. We opened initially, Dorothy tasted it and thought there was nothing to it. I tasted it and found an fine finessed finish on the back of my palette. My call was give it more time in the glass. This would have been a perfect wine for the decanter. By the end of the meal the wine huge and fabulous, a mountain with splendid terrain.
    The meal was a fine finish for an exciting day. Back in the condo for our last night in Tucson we all slept well.
