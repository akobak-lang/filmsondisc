Great Last  Day

January 5,  2000

Lauren and Liz.

We slipped from under the covers before the sun came up so Liz could warm up for her playoff for fifth or sixth place in The Copper Bowl 14s. This morning she warmed up with Lauren Lui from Texas, who is in the finals of the round of 16 playoffs.

Plukas and Liz after the match.

     Alexis Plukas was Lizzie's opponent in the battle for fifth place. Liz had beaten Plukas in a close three-setter at the National Hard Courts last month, so the older Plukas was out for some revenge. Plukas got out of the gate very quickly with a love game for a 1-0 lead, but Liz got right back in the match with some strong shot-making. Liz played a very solid first set and won 6-3. The second set was much like the first. Plukas hit her share of winners, but Liz controlled the points with an intelligent mix of shots. Slices and drop-shots were effective against Plukas and Liz controlled the flow of the match. Plukas managed her share of winners, but Liz prevailed 6-4 for a straight set victory and the fifth place Kuchina Doll trophy. The trophies are each hand made by local native Americans.

That's All Folks!

     Packing up at the condo was a light-hearted chore after Lizzie's success in Arizona. We are leaving for Vegas this evening for a few days in sin city. For our gourmet friends, they will be delighted to note that our lunch consisted of leftover salad and frozen pizza. No wine was within drinking distance. Since we had a few hours before checking in at the airport, we decided to head to Biosphere 2, the largest greenhouse in the world. The experimental compound is now run by Columbia University and was built by the Bass Brothers of Houston as a project to monitor human behavior in an enclosed. controlled environment. In the late eighties a group of eight participants spent two consecutive years in the Biosphere.

Liz looks at the Biosphere model at the beginning of the afternoon tour.

     I can't even imagine what it was like to spend two years confined within the habitats of the Biosphere? We spent two hours touring the place and it seemed like an eternity. We did not actually enter any of the habitats within the Biosphere, but we roamed through the living quarters where the project participants lived. The project was repeated for one six month cycle several years ago, but the primary function of the Biosphere project now focuses on how changes in the atmosphere affect plant life and in return affect the atmosphere. 

Listening to a lecture of humidity.

     Before heading into the main Biosphere, we visited several mini-habitats. Humid! We moved quickly through the flora and dodged some drops of humidity.

A marble dinner table inside the Biosphere 2.


    Well, anyway, we're in Las Vegas now staying in the lap of luxury at The Bellagio Hotel. Alex was here awaiting our arrival and we had a fabulous dinner at Lutece at the Venetian Hotel. The room is an architectural gem, though some of the spiral designs put Lizzie in  a slight tizzy until she changed her seat. There are three smiling Kobaks pictured below at the Bellagio garden courtyard before breakfast the next morning. The less said the better about the quality of Bellagio's breakfast.
